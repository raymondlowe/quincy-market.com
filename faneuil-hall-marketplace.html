Faneuil Hall Marketplace — the Four Buildings and What Is in Each

Tenant counts are the operator's own directory, read 21 November 2026. Shops and restaurants change, so check the operator's listings before you travel for one particular shop.

Faneuil Hall Marketplace is not one building. It is a block of four, built a century apart and doing four different jobs: Faneuil Hall (1742, the meeting hall, now a National Park Service visitor centre), Quincy Market (1826, the food hall), and the North Market and South Market buildings (completed 1827, the shopping rows either side of Quincy Market). The operator's directory names 74 businesses: 52 in the four buildings, 20 pushcarts under the two canopies, and two it files under West End.

That is the whole confusion in one sentence: Quincy Market is inside Faneuil Hall Marketplace and is not Faneuil Hall. One is a food hall; the other is a 1742 assembly room across the cobbles.

If you only want the answer: food is in Quincy Market, shops are in the North and South Market buildings, history is in Faneuil Hall, and the pushcarts are outside under the canopies at either end.

Faneuil Hall — the 1742 Building, Not the Food Hall

Faneuil Hall is the oldest building on the block and the only one the City of Boston owns and the National Park Service runs as a visitor site. Peter Faneuil built it in 1742 as a meeting hall with a market underneath, and it has been a public meeting room ever since. Entry is free on every floor.

The building has three public levels. The visitor centre is on the market floor, the Great Hall is the second-floor assembly room where the Ranger talks happen, and the Ancient and Honorable Artillery Company museum is on the fourth floor, generally open on weekdays 9:00–15:00.

The Great Hall also closes to the public when the City has permitted an event in it, so a closed door does not always mean a closed building. Ranger talks run Wednesday to Sunday at 10:30, 11:30, 12:30, 13:30, 14:30 and 15:30, about 15 minutes each, and they are free.

The building is closed on Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day. Access to the upper floors is by the ramp and lift on the south side of the building.

Quincy Market — the 1826 Food Hall

Quincy Market is the long granite hall in the middle of the block, built in 1826 and named for Mayor Josiah Quincy, who organised its construction without a tax or a debt. It is the one building everybody means when they say "Quincy Market", and it is where the food is.

Inside, 24 food counters run down both sides of a central aisle, from chowder and lobster rolls to gelato, poke and pizza. A second floor sits above the colonnade and is used for events and, in season, the Christmas Holiday Market. Three businesses belong to the building rather than the colonnade — Co-Operatives, Ned Devine's Irish Pub and The Salty Dog — which is why a sit-down meal can be had without leaving it.

Every entrance is stepped: four steps up from the cobbles at each temple-fronted end and at the central pavilion. Expect to queue at the counters between about 11:00 and 14:00, and to find seats more easily outside that window.

What each counter sells, and what a meal costs, is on the Quincy Market food guide; the layout of the hall itself is on what is inside Quincy Market.

North Market Building — Souvenirs, Comics and the Big Restaurant

The North Market Building is the row on the north side, completed in 1827 and now given over to retail. Thirteen tenants are listed in it, and they lean towards gifts, souvenirs and things for children: Best of Boston for Boston-themed gifts, I Love Boston Sports for team gear, Build-A-Bear, Newbury Comics, Christmas in Boston, Pepper Palace, Ben & Jerry's and Sunglass Hut among them.

Margaritaville, the full-service restaurant, sits at this end of the row, and it is the one tenant here that publishes opening hours of its own rather than the marketplace's. Outside, under the North Canopy, eight pushcarts sell jewellery, photographs, repairs and gifts from small independent traders.

South Market Building — Clothes, Shoes and the Big Names

The South Market Building faces it across Quincy Market, also completed in 1827, and carries the brands you would recognise from a high street: Coach, Urban Outfitters, Samsonite and The Black Dog, with IT'SUGAR for sweets and Sugar Factory for a sit-down dessert.

Two tenants here are worth knowing about if you have children with you: Putt Across America is an 18-hole indoor putting course with state-themed trivia, and Sloomoo Minimoo is a slime bar where you build your own. The South Canopy outside adds 12 more pushcarts, mostly art, jewellery and Boston souvenirs.

Hours, and How They Differ by Building

Three of the four buildings keep the marketplace's own hours: Monday to Saturday 10:00–21:00, Sunday 11:00–19:00. The operator adds that holiday, restaurant and retail hours may vary, and individual shops do open or shut early — the hours block is the marketplace's, not each tenant's.

Faneuil Hall is the exception, because it is run by the National Park Service to a federal schedule: in November and December 2026 it opens at 10:00 Wednesday to Sunday and is closed on Monday and Tuesday. A Monday visit to the marketplace therefore gets you Quincy Market and the shops but not the Great Hall.

Quick Answers

Is Quincy Market the same as Faneuil Hall?

No. Quincy Market is the 1826 food hall in the middle of the block; Faneuil Hall is the separate 1742 meeting hall at the west end, run by the National Park Service. Both sit inside Faneuil Hall Marketplace, together with the North and South Market buildings. The full comparison is on Quincy Market vs Faneuil Hall.

How many buildings are in Faneuil Hall Marketplace?

Four: Faneuil Hall, Quincy Market, the North Market Building and the South Market Building. The operator also lists two canopy pushcart groups and two businesses under "West End".

Where do I eat?

Quincy Market, for all 24 food counters and three sit-down businesses. Margaritaville is in the North Market Building and Sugar Factory in the South Market Building.

Where are the shops?

The North and South Market buildings. Souvenirs and gifts lean north; clothing, shoes and the larger brands lean south; the pushcarts are under the canopies outside.

Is Faneuil Hall free?

Yes — admission is free on all floors, and the Ranger talks are free. It is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays in November and December 2026.

Which entrance do I use?

Quincy Market has three stepped entrances: the two temple-fronted ends and the central pavilion. All are four steps up from the cobbles.
