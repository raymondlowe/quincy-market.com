How to Get to Quincy Market — T, Parking, Logan Airport & Walking Guide

Last updated: October 2026. Transport, parking rates and hours shown are the typical 2026–27 ranges and may change — confirm MBTA schedules and garage rates before travelling.

Quincy Market is in downtown Boston, inside the Faneuil Hall Marketplace between Faneuil Hall Square and Commercial Street, next to the Haymarket and Government Center neighbourhoods. It is free to enter, sits directly on the Freedom Trail, and three MBTA T stops are within one block — which makes it one of the easiest destinations in Boston to reach without a car. This page covers every leg of the journey: by T, by commuter rail, from Logan Airport, by car and parking, and on foot.

Quick facts: Free admission · 4 MBTA T stops within 1 block (Green, Blue, Orange lines) · Official parking at 75 State Street Garage, entrance 5 Broad St (24/7) · Free Massport shuttle from Logan Airport to the Blue Line · On the Freedom Trail · Contact (617) 523-1300.

Getting Here by MBTA T

The T is the fastest and cheapest way to reach Quincy Market, and you have three lines to choose from — all of them within a one-block walk of the market doors:

Line Nearest stop Walk to Quincy Market
Blue Line Aquarium ~3 minutes — exit on State Street and walk north past the harbor to Faneuil Hall Square
Green Line Haymarket ~2 minutes — the stop sits at the edge of Haymarket, directly beside the market
Green Line Gov't Center ~4 minutes — walk down Court Street through the plaza toward Faneuil Hall
Orange Line State St / Haymarket ~3 minutes — the stop is effectively paired with Haymarket on the same block
Red Line Park Street ~10–12 minutes — walk north up Washington Street / Winter Street to Faneuil Hall Square; a longer walk but useful if you are already downtown
Commuter Rail North Station ~10–12 minutes — walk east along Friend Street and Commercial Street to the market; also connects to the Green and Orange lines at Haymarket

If you are staying anywhere along the Green, Blue or Orange lines, ride to one of the four stops above and you will be on foot for less than five minutes. The Green Line's Haymarket and Gov't Center stops both serve the market from the west; the Blue Line's Aquarium stop approaches from the south-east along State Street; the Orange Line's State St/Haymarket stop links in from the south. The Red Line (Park Street) and Commuter Rail (North Station) are alternatives when those lines are more convenient, but expect a 10–12 minute walk rather than a 2–4 minute one.

From Logan Airport

Flying in? The journey from Logan to Quincy Market is straightforward and does not require a taxi:

  1. Take the free Massport shuttle from your terminal to the Blue Line's Airport Station. The shuttles run frequently and cost nothing.
  2. Board the Blue Line at Airport Station and ride three stops toward downtown Boston.
  3. Get off at Aquarium and walk ~3 minutes north to Faneuil Hall Square and Quincy Market.

The whole trip — shuttle plus three T stops plus a short walk — takes roughly 25–35 minutes door to door. A taxi or rideshare from Logan to the market is an alternative for late-night flights or if you are travelling with heavy luggage, but it is not necessary for the journey itself.

Parking at Quincy Market

Driving is an option, but parking around Faneuil Hall Marketplace is metered and limited on weekdays. There are three garages to know about:

Garage Address Rate Notes
75 State Street Garage (official) Entrance 5 Broad St ~$41 weekday (verify current rate) Open 24/7; closest garage to the market
Dock Square Garage 20 Clinton St ~$29 weekday / $25 weekend (verify current rate) One block from the market; weekend rate is lower
Haymarket Center Garage 136 Blackstone St Check garage board or app Closest to Haymarket T stop; good for T riders who still want a car

The official garage for the Faneuil Hall Marketplace is the 75 State Street Garage — entrance at 5 Broad Street, open 24 hours, seven days a week. It is the safest bet for a full day at the market because it never closes. Weekday rates are higher (~$41) than weekends; confirm the current posted rate when you arrive.

Best and Worst Times to Park

Parking in downtown Boston is a waiting game. The times below are based on the typical weekday and weekend flow around the Faneuil Hall Marketplace:

  • Avoid weekday 11:00–14:00 and 16:30–18:30. These are the two worst windows — the lunch rush and the evening commute. Garages fill and rates are at their peak.
  • Avoid Saturday 12:00–16:00. Saturday midday is the busiest parking period of the week, driven by tourists and weekend shoppers.
  • Easiest times: weekday before 10:00, weekday after 19:00, and Sunday before noon. The garages are emptier, the walk is quieter, and the market itself is less crowded.

If you are coming for a specific meal, work the parking around the market's own opening hours rather than the city's: the Faneuil Hall Marketplace opens Mon–Sat 10:00–21:00 and Sun 11:00–19:00, and the Quincy Market food hall opens later on Sunday (noon–18:00). Arriving at opening means a space and a quiet first look at the stalls.

Walking to Quincy Market

Quincy Market is a pleasant walk from several of Boston's most visited neighbourhoods, and it sits on the Freedom Trail — the red-brick walking route that links the city's historic sites:

  • From the North End (~10–12 minutes): cross the bridge at Hanover Street toward the harbor, then follow the signs to Faneuil Hall Square. This is the classic approach through Boston's historic Italian district.
  • From the Waterfront (~8–10 minutes): walk west along Atlantic Avenue or Congress Street until you reach Faneuil Hall Square. You pass the Boston Harborwalk and the New England Aquarium en route.
  • From the Old State House / Downtown (~5–7 minutes): walk north along State Street or Congress Street to the market. The market sits directly behind the Old State House, one of the Freedom Trail's anchor stops.
  • Along the Freedom Trail: Quincy Market is a marked stop between Faneuil Hall (the previous stop) and the Old State House (the next stop). If you are following the trail, you will pass through the market naturally.

Because the market sits at the intersection of four T lines, two major walking routes and the Freedom Trail, it is genuinely one of the easiest destinations in Boston to reach without a car. If you are staying in downtown Boston, the walk is often faster and less stressful than hunting for parking. See our hotels near Quincy Market guide for lodging in the North End, Waterfront and Government Center neighbourhoods — all within a 10–15 minute walk.

Money-saving tip: Pre-book parking through SpotHero or Pilgrim Parking and compare Dock Square vs Haymarket Center rates before you drive. The ~$29 weekday Dock Square rate is often cheaper than the official garage — but it is a slightly longer walk to the market entrance.

Hours & Admission

Admission to Quincy Market and the Faneuil Hall Marketplace is free — there is no ticket and no entry fee. The marketplace and its shops open and close on the following schedule:

  • Faneuil Hall Marketplace: Mon–Sat 10:00–21:00, Sun 11:00–19:00.
  • Quincy Market food hall: closes earlier on Sunday — Sun 12:00–18:00 (food hall specifically). Check the current day's hours at the entrance if you are planning a late-afternoon Sunday visit.
Hours note: The Faneuil Hall Marketplace's official schedule (Mon–Sat 10–9, Sun 11–7) governs the whole complex. The Quincy Market food hall has a shorter Sunday window (noon–6pm) according to multiple visitor sources. If you are coming for food on a Sunday, aim for early afternoon rather than late.

Practical Tips

  • Public transport first. Four T stops within one block means you rarely need a car. Save the parking hassle and the ~$41 weekday rate by taking the T.
  • Arrive at opening. Whether by T or car, arriving when the market opens gives you the shortest queues, the best food, and the quietest experience.
  • Peak lunch is 11:00–14:00. The food hall is shoulder-to-shoulder at midday, especially on weekends. If you want a seat, come before 11:00 or after 14:00.
  • Street performers are part of the draw. The sword swallowers and jugglers on the cobblestones outside are a genuine Boston attraction — budget extra time if you want to watch.
  • Watch your bag. The food hall is crowded and the pickpocket risk is low but not zero; keep wallets in front pockets.
Quick summary: Take the T (Blue Line to Aquarium, Green Line to Haymarket or Gov't Center, Orange Line to State St/Haymarket) — four stops within one block. From Logan Airport, ride the free Massport shuttle to the Blue Line's Airport Station, then three stops to Aquarium. If you drive, the official 75 State Street Garage (entrance 5 Broad St, 24/7) is the safest bet; Dock Square at 20 Clinton St is cheaper on weekdays (~$29). Free admission, on the Freedom Trail, and contact (617) 523-1300 for current information.
