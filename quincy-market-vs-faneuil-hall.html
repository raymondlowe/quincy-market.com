Is Quincy Market in Faneuil Hall? What the Difference Is

Last updated: November 2026. The building facts below do not move; the tenant counts and the National Park Service hours do, so both carry the date they were read. Confirm before you travel.

No. Quincy Market is in Faneuil Hall Marketplace but not in Faneuil Hall. Quincy Market is the 1826 granite food hall in the middle of the block; Faneuil Hall is the separate 1742 meeting hall at the west end, owned by the City of Boston and run as a visitor center and historic site by the National Park Service.

One-line answer: Quincy Market is the 1826 food-hall building in the middle of the block. Faneuil Hall is the separate 1742 meeting hall at the west end of the block — City-owned, NPS-interpreted, free to enter. Faneuil Hall Marketplace is the marketplace around Quincy Market: the North and South Market buildings with their shops, restaurants and offices.

The confusion runs both ways: people call the whole marketplace "Faneuil Hall", and others assume the Hall is inside it. Both habits point at different buildings. Here is what each one is.

The third name is the one that causes most of the trouble. Faneuil Hall Marketplace is the marketplace around Quincy Market — the North and South Market buildings with their shops, restaurants and offices. Faneuil Hall stands next door to it and is not part of the leased property: the marketplace operator runs the shops, restaurants and offices, while the National Park Service runs the Hall. The full history of both buildings is on our history page.

Which one, if you only have one choice

Most visitors do both, because the two stand at opposite ends of one short block. If you genuinely have to pick, the choice is food or history.

Go to Quincy Market if you want food

Quincy Market is where the food is. The counters line one central aisle down the length of the building and the sit-down restaurants sit in the buildings around it, so a counter meal happens under one roof. On 9 November 2026 the operator's tenant directory listed 24 food counters in the Quincy Market Food Colonnade, out of 74 tenants in seven groups.

If you searched for Faneuil Hall restaurants, this is the building you want. The operator's restaurant category listed four full-service restaurants on 13 October 2026 — among them an Irish pub, a seafood grill and a Margaritaville — and they are around Quincy Market rather than in the food colonnade. The current lineup is on our restaurants and eateries page; what to order once you are inside is on best food at Quincy Market.

Go to Faneuil Hall if you want history — what is in Faneuil Hall

Faneuil Hall was first constructed in 1742 — Peter Faneuil's gift to the town — and it is a National Historic Landmark on the Freedom Trail. The building is the history: the National Park Service visitor center is on the ground floor and the Great Hall is on the second floor.

In this hall Samuel Adams, John Hancock, James Otis and others spoke against the Sugar and Stamp Acts — the meetings that made Faneuil Hall the "Cradle of Liberty". Free ranger talks run Wednesday–Sunday in the Great Hall at 10:30, 11:30, 12:30, 13:30, 14:30 and 15:30 (about 15 minutes each) — the hall is closed to visitors on Mondays and, on the current 1 November–31 December 2026 schedule, on Tuesdays as well.

On the same schedule the Great Hall is open 10:00–16:30 Wednesday to Sunday and the visitor center on the ground floor is open 10:00–17:00 on the same days. The Great Hall also closes to visitors when the City has permitted an event there.

There is no Faneuil Hall directory of shops, because the shops are not in the building. The directory people usually mean is the marketplace's tenant list — the shops in the North and South Market buildings — which our shopping page walks through.

If you only have an hour

If you only have an hour, spend it in Quincy Market. Walk the central aisle from one temple front to the other, pick a counter, and eat in the rotunda under the dome — that is the part of the block you cannot get anywhere else. Cross to Faneuil Hall only if the day and the clock allow: it is open Wednesday to Sunday, the Great Hall shuts at 16:30, and a ranger talk takes about 15 minutes. Skip the shops.

Doing both in one visit

The order below puts the building with the closing time first, so you never arrive at a locked door. It works Wednesday to Sunday on the current schedule.

  1. Check the day first. Faneuil Hall's Great Hall is closed to visitors on Mondays and, on the current 1 November–31 December 2026 schedule, on Tuesdays as well — on those days do the market and skip the hall.
  2. Start at Faneuil Hall when it opens at 10:00. The visitor center on the ground floor and the Great Hall on the second floor are both open; take the 10:30 ranger talk, which is free and needs no ticket.
  3. Cross the cobblestones to Quincy Market for lunch. Walk the length of the central aisle before you choose, because the counters run down both sides — then order and eat in the rotunda under the dome.
  4. Shop the North and South Market buildings after lunch. Apparel, Boston souvenirs, specialty gifts, accessories, shoes, toys and locally owned stores, including college and team gear.
  5. Finish with dessert and the street performers. The dessert and coffee counters are in the food hall; the performers work the cobblestone promenades daily from 11:00 to 21:00 — jugglers, acrobats, musicians and stunt acts, all approved by the marketplace's management.
The one thing to get right: the hall's hours are not the market's. The marketplace's published hours are Monday to Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00, and the operator adds that holiday, restaurant and retail hours may vary. The food-hall counters publish no hours of their own, so treat those as the building's hours, not each stall's. Current hours for both buildings are on our Quincy Market hours page.

Getting there

The marketplace is on Boston's Freedom Trail. The operator says it is a short walk from the State, Haymarket and Government Center MBTA stations: State is on the Blue and Orange lines, Haymarket on the Green and Orange, and Government Center on the Green and Blue. Aquarium, on the Blue Line, is a few minutes further. Our public transport guide has the step-by-step version.

From Logan, take the free Massport on-airport shuttle — routes 22, 33, 55 or 66 — to Airport station on the Blue Line. From there it is two stops to Aquarium and three to State.

If you are driving, the garage marketed as the official Faneuil Hall Marketplace garage is the 75 State Street Garage, with its entrance at 5 Broad Street — a label that comes from the parking reseller, not from the marketplace operator, which lists several garages within a 15-minute walk and calls none of them official.
