Quincy Market Boston 2027 — Complete Food Guide with Price List

Last updated: October 2026. Prices are the typical 2026–27 range for the Quincy Market food hall and the surrounding Faneuil Hall Marketplace; individual stall prices change with the season — always confirm at the counter before ordering.

Quincy Market is the 27,000 sq ft historic food hall inside Faneuil Hall Marketplace in downtown Boston — opened in 1826 by Mayor Josiah Quincy and free to enter. It is the single best place in the city to eat like a local on a budget: a full meal costs well under $20, and you can walk between 35+ stalls under one roof.

This guide leads with what things actually cost, then the four dishes worth queuing for, the 2026–27 vendor lineup, the status of the $18.26 bicentennial specials, and the practical tips that keep you out of the worst crowds.

Price Bands at a Glance

Quincy Market has two ways to eat: the ground-floor food hall stalls, where you order at a counter and take a seat in the colonnade, and the sit-down restaurants and pubs around the perimeter. Here is the typical 2026–27 cost per person:

Where to eat Price per person (2026–27) What you get
Food hall stall (counter service) $8–15 One substantial item — a clam chowder bread bowl, a sandwich, a pasta bowl, a poke bowl, a gyro, or a slice with a drink.
Sit-down restaurant / pub $10–18 A plated meal with table service — seafood, thin-crust pizza, Irish pub fare or a Mexican entrée, plus a drink.
Bottom line: budget $15–18 per person and you will eat very well. That is the price of a single entrée in many sit-down Boston restaurants a block away — which is exactly why the food hall is packed at peak hours.

The Must-Eat List — Four Dishes, No Regrets

Walk the colonnade long enough and you will see the same four orders repeated by people who know Boston. Order these and you have done Quincy Market correctly.

1. Clam Chowder — Boston Chowda Co.

Boston clam chowder is a cream-based soup of clams, diced potatoes and onion. Crushed crackers go on top as a garnish, not as the thickener — it is thicker than the clear Rhode Island version and nothing like red Manhattan chowder. In the Quincy Market Food Colonnade, Boston Chowda Co. is the stall to order it from; ask for it in a sourdough bread bowl. It falls inside the $8–15 counter-service band and is easily split between two people.

2. Lobster Roll

A Maine-style lobster roll is chilled lobster meat dressed lightly and served in a grilled split-top bun — claw and knuckle meat, not a mayo-heavy filler. It is the priciest single item in the food hall and sits toward the top of the market's per-person range, so if you are counting dollars, split one and add a chowder rather than ordering two.

3. Boston Cream Pie

Despite the name, Boston cream pie is a cake: two layers of sponge with vanilla custard or cream filling under a chocolate glaze. It is believed to have been invented at Boston's Parker House Hotel in 1856, originally as the "Parker House Chocolate Cream Pie." Look for it at the bakery and dessert stalls in the colonnade — offerings rotate, so check the counter on the day of your visit.

4. Baked Beans & Brown Bread

Boston brown bread is not a standard loaf. It is a slightly sweet steamed bread, sweetened with molasses or golden syrup and studded with raisins, traditionally steamed in a coffee can — which is why it comes out round. It is served with home-made baked beans, and together they are the city's oldest comfort food: a market-wide New England classic rather than one specific stall, so look for it among the traditional New England counters and confirm the serving stall on the day.

Pro tip: split one chowder bread bowl between two people and add a dessert or a lobster roll. That keeps the per-person bill at the low end of the band and leaves room for a second stop elsewhere in the marketplace.

Practical Tips — When to Go and How to Avoid the Crowds

Peak times to avoid

  • Peak lunch is 11:00–14:00. This is when the food hall is shoulder-to-shoulder and the popular stalls have real queues — worst on Saturdays and on any summer weekday.
  • Weekend middays and school-holiday afternoons are the next-busiest windows; the colonnade seating fills first.

Best times to visit

  • Weekday mornings before 11:00 — quiet stalls, short queues, and you can actually find a seat.
  • Weekday evenings after 19:00 — the lunch and after-work crowds have gone.
  • Sunday before the lunch rush — but note the food hall typically closes earlier on Sunday than the rest of the marketplace, so plan an early lunch rather than a late-afternoon visit.

Practical notes

  • Admission is free. There is no ticket and no entry fee to Quincy Market or the wider Faneuil Hall Marketplace.
  • Three MBTA T stops are within a block: Government Center and Haymarket (Green Line) and Aquarium (Blue Line). See our Quincy Market hours, T directions and Logan Airport guide for the full journey.
  • Driving in? Read Quincy Market parking for the official garages, rates and where to park.
  • Confirm hours before you travel. The marketplace is generally open Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00, but the food hall's Sunday hours can be shorter.

Money-saving tips

  • Split a chowder bread bowl. It is the best-value single item in the building and easily feeds two as part of a bigger meal.
  • Eat at the counter stalls, not the sit-down rooms, to stay at the low end of the per-person band.
  • Skip the premium lobster roll if you are budgeting — pair a chowder with a cheaper second item instead.
Quick summary: budget $15–18 per person, order clam chowder in a bread bowl, split it if you can, add a lobster roll or a slice of Boston cream pie, and arrive before 11:00 or after 19:00 to skip the worst crowds. Free admission, three T stops within a block, and the whole thing sits on the Freedom Trail.
