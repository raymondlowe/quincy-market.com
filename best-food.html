Quincy Market Boston 2027 — Complete Food Guide with Price List

Last updated: October 2026. Prices are the typical 2026–27 range for the Quincy Market food hall and the surrounding Faneuil Hall Marketplace; individual stall prices change with the season — always confirm at the counter before ordering.

Quincy Market is the 27,000 sq ft historic food hall inside Faneuil Hall Marketplace in downtown Boston — opened in 1826 by Mayor Josiah Quincy and free to enter. It is the single best place in the city to eat like a local on a budget: a full meal costs well under $20, and you can walk between 35+ stalls under one roof.

This guide leads with what things actually cost, then the four dishes worth queuing for, the 2026–27 vendor lineup, the status of the $18.26 bicentennial specials, and the practical tips that keep you out of the worst crowds.

Price Bands at a Glance

Quincy Market has two ways to eat: the ground-floor food hall stalls, where you order at a counter and take a seat in the colonnade, and the sit-down restaurants and pubs around the perimeter. Here is the typical 2026–27 cost per person:

Where to eat Price per person (2026–27) What you get
Food hall stall (counter service) $8–15 One substantial item — a clam chowder bread bowl, a sandwich, a pasta bowl, a poke bowl, a gyro, or a slice with a drink.
Sit-down restaurant / pub $10–18 A plated meal with table service — seafood, thin-crust pizza, Irish pub fare or a Mexican entrée, plus a drink.
Dessert, coffee, snack $4–9 Gelato, cookies, bubble tea, a coffee, or a candy-shop treat.
Bottom line: budget $15–18 per person and you will eat very well. That is the price of a single entrée in many sit-down Boston restaurants a block away — which is exactly why the food hall is packed at peak hours.

The Must-Eat List — Four Dishes, No Regrets

Walk the colonnade long enough and you will see the same four orders repeated by people who know Boston. Order these and you have done Quincy Market correctly.

1. Clam Chowder — Boston Chowda Co.

Boston clam chowder is a cream-based soup of clams, diced potatoes and onion, thickened with a flour-based roux. It is thicker than the clear Rhode Island version and nothing like red Manhattan chowder. Crushed crackers go on top as a garnish, not as the thickener. In the Quincy Market Food Colonnade, Boston Chowda Co. — a two-time chowder-contest winner — is the stall to order it from; ask for it in a sourdough bread bowl (about $10–14). It sits at the top of the $8–15 counter-service band and is easily split between two people.

2. Lobster Roll — Wicked Lobsta or Boston & Maine Fish Company

A Maine-style lobster roll is chilled lobster meat dressed lightly and served in a grilled split-top bun — claw and knuckle meat, not a mayo-heavy filler. In the Food Colonnade, Wicked Lobsta and Boston & Maine Fish Company both serve them, at roughly $16–20. It is the priciest single item in the food hall and sits at the top of the market's per-person range, so if you are counting dollars, split one and add a chowder rather than ordering two.

3. Boston Cream Pie

Despite the name, Boston cream pie is a cake: two layers of sponge with vanilla custard or cream filling under a chocolate glaze. It is believed to have been invented at Boston's Parker House Hotel in 1856, originally as the "Parker House Chocolate Cream Pie." Look for it at the bakery and dessert stalls in the colonnade (about $5–9); offerings rotate, so check the counter on the day of your visit. For a cold alternative, Joey's Gelateria serves chocolate-topped gelato.

4. Baked Beans & Brown Bread

Boston brown bread is not a standard loaf. It is a slightly sweet steamed bread, sweetened with molasses or golden syrup and studded with raisins, traditionally steamed in a coffee can — which is why it comes out round. It is served with home-made baked beans, and together they are the city's oldest comfort food. This is a market-wide New England classic rather than one specific stall; look for it among the traditional New England counters such as North End Bakery, and confirm the serving stall on the day (about $7–11 for the plate).

Pro tip: split one chowder bread bowl between two people and add a dessert or a lobster roll. That keeps the per-person bill at the low end of the band and leaves room for a second stop elsewhere in the marketplace.

The $18.26 Bicentennial Specials — Status for 2027

Quincy Market opened in 1826, and in 2026 — its bicentennial year — the marketplace marked the anniversary with "The 1826 Special": a collection of themed food and shopping items priced at exactly $18.26. It launched on 26 August 2026 and, per the operator's announcements, ran through the end of 2026 (31 December). Participating vendors across the collection included Bakey, North End Bakery, Boston & Maine, Wicked Lobsta, Boston Chowda, The Berry Twist, The Monkey Bar, Ben & Jerry's, Vivi Bubble Tea, Wicked Dog, Sloomoo, Boston Logos, Putt Across America and Co-Operatives.

Do the $18.26 specials run in 2027? No. The only published window ended on 31 December 2026, and no extension into 2027 has been announced — the operator's bicentennial event page is now dated to the single anniversary date (26 August 2026). Treat the $18.26 price as a 2026 bicentennial promotion that has finished, and budget from the regular price bands above. If the $18.26 deal is the reason for your trip, check faneuilhallmarketplace.com in case the operator re-runs it.

Why it matters: the $18.26 price was a bicentennial promotion, not a permanent menu price. The standard per-person bands in this guide are what you should plan around.

Practical Tips — When to Go and How to Avoid the Crowds

Peak times to avoid

  • Peak lunch is 11:00–14:00. This is when the food hall is shoulder-to-shoulder and the popular stalls (Boston Chowda, Wicked Lobsta) have real queues — worst on Saturdays and on any summer weekday.
  • Weekend middays and school-holiday afternoons are the next-busiest windows; the colonnade seating fills first.

Best times to visit

  • Weekday mornings before 11:00 — quiet stalls, short queues, and you can actually find a seat.
  • Weekday evenings after 19:00 — the lunch and after-work crowds have gone.
  • Sunday before the lunch rush — but note the food hall typically closes earlier on Sunday than the rest of the marketplace, so plan an early lunch rather than a late-afternoon visit.

Practical notes

  • Admission is free. There is no ticket and no entry fee to Quincy Market or the wider Faneuil Hall Marketplace.
  • Hours — and the Sunday caveat. The marketplace as a whole is open Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00 (the operator's published schedule). The Quincy Market food-hall stalls keep shorter hours on Sunday — the operator's own visitor guidance lists a noon–18:00 window for Sunday — so plan an early lunch and confirm the day's hours at the entrance.
  • Three MBTA T stops are within a block: Government Center and Haymarket (Green Line) and Aquarium (Blue Line). See our Quincy Market hours, T directions and Logan Airport guide for the full journey.
  • Driving in? Read Quincy Market parking for the official garages, rates and where to park.

Money-saving tips

  • Split a chowder bread bowl. At $10–14 it is the best-value single item in the building and easily feeds two as part of a bigger meal.
  • Eat at the counter stalls, not the sit-down rooms, to stay at the low end of the per-person band.
  • Skip the premium lobster roll if you are budgeting — pair a chowder with a cheaper second item (a Dog House sausage at $7–11, or a KongDog at $6–10) instead.
Quick summary: budget $15–18 per person, order clam chowder in a bread bowl, split it if you can, add a lobster roll or a slice of Boston cream pie, and arrive before 11:00 or after 19:00 to skip the worst crowds. Free admission, three T stops within a block, and the whole thing sits on the Freedom Trail.
