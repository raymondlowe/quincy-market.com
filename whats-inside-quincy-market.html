What's Inside Quincy Market — Food Hall, Stalls and Vendors

Last updated: November 2026. The roster and the hours are the two things that move, so both carry the date they were read. Check the operator's own pages before you travel.

What is inside Quincy Market is one indoor food hall: a single granite building with food stalls and vendors on both sides of a central aisle. You step in off the cobblestones — up four steps at either temple-fronted end or at the front of the central pavilion — and the hall, the stalls and the vendors are all under the roof. The one thing first-timers get wrong is the name. Quincy Market is the food hall; Faneuil Hall is the brick meeting hall from 1742 next door, and it does not sell food.

Quick facts: Indoor food hall, opened 1826 · 535 feet long, one central aisle and counters on both sides · 24 counters in the operator's directory on 9 November 2026 · four table-service restaurants across the three buildings · no ticket, no entry fee · Mon–Sat 10am–9pm, Sun 11am–7pm.

What you walk into

Quincy Market is the long granite building in the middle of Faneuil Hall Marketplace, completed in 1826 to Alexander Parris's design, 535 feet long and 50 feet wide.

The counters are indoors. What is outdoors is the two pedestrian malls either side of the building — North Market Street and South Market Street, both closed to vehicles — where the pushcarts, the street performers and the summer restaurant patios are.

One central aisle, counters on both sides

The food counters run down an enclosed central hall 512 feet long and 14 feet high, divided by two rows of cast-iron Doric columns — 59 in each row — with vendors' stalls on both sides of the center aisle.

Because the counters line one central aisle, the hall is in front of you as soon as you are through the doors: the food is on both sides of you, and the length of the building is the length of the choice.

Halfway along, the dome and the rotunda

Halfway along, the hall opens up under a copper-sheathed elliptical dome 70 by 50 feet on an octagonal drum; the rotunda below it is the building's gathering space.

The entrances — four steps at each

The three main entrances — both temple-fronted ends and the front of the central pavilion — are each up a flight of four steps.

One temple front faces Faneuil Hall across the cobblestones and the other closes the far end of the building; the central pavilion door is the middle entrance on the long side.

Outside the doors: two malls and three other buildings

Everything else in the complex is a separate building: North Market and South Market are the granite-faced ranges flanking Quincy Market, and Faneuil Hall, the brick meeting hall from 1742, stands at the west end.

Built for stalls in 1826

The complex dates from 1826, when the market opened with 128 food stalls on the ground floor and exhibition space above; the City's 1996 designation report records that it reopened on 26 August 1976, its 150th anniversary, as one of the first "festival marketplaces" in the nation.

The operator calls Quincy Market "the largest and oldest food hall in the country" and "the first food hall in the United States" — its own marketing, not an independently measured claim.

The current roster is the operator's own tenant directory, which is the page to check on the day you go.

The operator's own numbers for the hall do not agree with each other — 24 counters in its directory, 30 food entries in its dining index, and "over 25 local eateries" on its homepage — so any count is worth only as much as the page it was counted on.

The four sit-down restaurants, by building

Four table-service restaurants are listed, and they are spread across all three buildings: Ned Devine's Irish Pub and The Salty Dog in the Quincy Market building, Margaritaville in the North Market building, and Sugar Factory in the South Market building.

Food is concentrated in the Quincy Market building. The North and South Market buildings are mostly shops, with one restaurant each (the North Market building also lists Ben & Jerry's).

No stall numbers, and no plan to put them on

The operator publishes no floor plan, no stall numbers and no counter-by-counter location, so no counter can be placed at a numbered spot in the hall.

Hours and access

Hours as published by Faneuil Hall Marketplace, read on 9 November 2026:

What Hours
Quincy Market food hall (all counters)Mon–Sat 10am–9pm · Sun 11am–7pm
Margaritaville (North Market building restaurant)Daily 11am–midnight

The marketplace publishes one building-wide hours block for the whole complex, with the warning that "holiday, restaurant, and retail hours may vary".

The counters do not publish hours of their own: their pages carry that same building-wide block. The restaurant Margaritaville does publish its own — daily, 11am to midnight — which is a reminder that a restaurant's hours are not the hall's.

There is no 2026 closure or special-hours notice: the operator's 2026 holiday programming is published as events, not as hours.

Steps, step-free access and restrooms

All three main entrances are up four steps, as described above. The operator publishes no accessible-route information and directs access questions to its front desk, frontdesk@fanhallmarketplace.com or 617-523-1300 x7047.

The operator publishes no restroom or ATM locations for Quincy Market; the only official restroom statement nearby is the Park Service's, for Faneuil Hall next door, where bathrooms are in the basement and on the second floor.

There is no seating plan and no seat count published either: the rotunda under the dome is the space the operator calls the building's gathering space, and nothing in the hall can be reserved ahead.

What's not there

The hall is a market rather than a restaurant, and several common expectations do not survive the walk in. These are the ones worth knowing before you go.

What people expect What is actually there
A ticket or an entry feeNone. There is no admission ticket and no published entry fee for the market itself
A reservationNo reservation path for any counter, and no booking anywhere in the dining listings
Table service throughoutFour table-service restaurants, listed above; everything else in the food hall is ordered at the counter, not from a table
Everything shut at 9pmThe building block is Mon–Sat 10am–9pm and Sun 11am–7pm; Margaritaville runs daily to midnight; the counters publish no hours of their own
A numbered stall planNo floor plan, no stall numbers and no counter-by-counter location is published

Beyond that, the operator sells one thing directly: a prepaid group meal voucher, accepted at participating merchants. Some experiences inside the buildings — escape rooms, mini-golf, pottery classes — are separately ticketed, but the operator publishes no prices for them.

Quincy Market is not inside Faneuil Hall. It is a separate 1826 building next door, and Faneuil Hall — the 1742 brick meeting hall at the west end — "does not sell food", as the National Park Service puts it.

The Great Hall that visitors read about is the room on the second floor of Faneuil Hall next door, not a room in Quincy Market.
