Quincy Market at Night — What Is Open After Dark

Last checked: 13 October 2026. The marketplace’s published hours are the fixed part of this page; individual restaurants, pubs and shops set their own closing times and the operator says those hours may vary, so confirm with the venue you have in mind. Prices are the vendors’ own published menu figures as of October 2026.

“Quincy Market at night” needs one fact up front, because it decides your whole evening: the marketplace closes at 9:00 p.m. Monday to Saturday and 7:00 p.m. on Sunday. The food colonnade, the shops and the pushcarts shut then, and the cobblestone square goes quiet. So the evening splits in two — the last two hours before closing, which are the best hours in the market, and the stretch afterwards, which belongs to the pubs and restaurants around the building rather than to the hall itself.

Plan around this: the colonnade closes with the market at 21:00 (19:00 Sunday), so if you want dinner at the counters, eat before 20:00 — after closing, dinner means one of the sit-down rooms. The free show — the street performers on the cobblestones — runs daily from 11:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m., so the last hour before closing is the one time of day you get the market with a crowd watching rather than a crowd queueing.

Two useful consequences of that schedule. First, the last hour is the quietest hour: the 11:00–14:00 crush is long gone, the after-work drinkers have not arrived at the pubs yet, and you can get a seat in the food hall without hovering for one. Second, the square is the free attraction: the market’s own description is of cobblestone promenades that come alive with music and seasonal programming, from street performers to live entertainment, and the performers’ published window runs right up to closing.

The value split is stark and worth knowing before you sit down. A counter meal in the colonnade typically costs $8–15 for a substantial item, and the cheapest single items — a hot dog at the sausage counters, a chowder bread bowl at Boston Chowda for $7.49 — are under $10. A plated main in one of the four rooms above starts around $19 and runs to $33, or roughly $30–45 per head with a drink. If you want an evening in the market and dinner under $20, the colonnade is the answer and the sit-down rooms are not.

Drinks After the Shops Close

The bars inside the marketplace are the practical answer to the 9:00 p.m. closing time, and the published prices are unusually easy to compare:

  • Cocktails at Ned Devine’s: $14–18 on the vendor’s own menu, in the Quincy Market Building.
  • Beer at Margaritaville: $8.75–10.25, in the North Market Building — the cheapest listed drink in the complex.
  • The Salty Dog: a seafood restaurant with a sit-down bar, in the Quincy Market Building — a table-service option if you want dinner rather than a tray.
  • Tree House Brewing Company: a craft-beer operation with outdoor seating in the West End. It is listed by the operator as a summer pop-up, so do not count on it for a winter evening — check whether it is trading before you walk over.

Beyond the marketplace walls, the obvious evening continuations are a short walk: the North End is about 10–12 minutes away on foot and is where Boston eats late, the waterfront is 8–10 minutes, and Downtown Crossing is 5–7 minutes. All three are reachable without a car, which matters at 10:00 p.m.

If You Are Coming for the Lights

The one period when Quincy Market is genuinely a night-time destination in its own right is the Christmas season. The marketplace plants a large tree in the West End between Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market, lights it at a free public ceremony in late November, and keeps it up through the holidays. In some years the trees on the market’s open spaces have also run Blink!, a light-and-sound show using more than 350,000 LEDs — but the show has skipped years, so treat it as a bonus rather than a fixture. Dates, timings and the current status of the light show are documented on our Christmas at Quincy Market page.

Getting Home After Dark

  • Three T stops are within a block: Haymarket and Government Center on the Green Line, Aquarium on the Blue Line, and State/Haymarket on the Orange Line. That is the reason people eat here in the evening at all — you are never more than a few minutes from a platform. The MBTA does not run a 24-hour subway, so check the last service on your line before committing to a late dinner.
  • Driving: the official garage for the marketplace is 75 State Street Garage, entrance at 5 Broad Street, and it is open 24 hours, seven days a week — the safe choice for an evening that runs past the last train. Rates and the alternatives, including Dock Square at 20 Clinton Street, are on our parking page.
  • Staying nearby: several hotels sit within a two-to-eight-minute walk — The Bostonian at 26 North Street (about 2 minutes), Canopy by Hilton at 99 Blackstone Street (4), Harborside Inn at 185 State Street (4), Hyatt Centric Faneuil Hall and Boston Marriott Long Wharf (5 each), and The Dagny, Club Quarters Faneuil Hall and Omni Parker House (7–8). Walking distance to your bed removes the evening-transport problem entirely; see the hotels near Quincy Market guide.

On safety, the honest version: the marketplace and its square are busy, well-lit and full of people for as long as the performers are working, and the surrounding blocks are on the Freedom Trail, which keeps foot traffic up in the evening. Keep the usual city precautions around the colonnade at peak times — it is crowded enough that bags should be kept in front of you — and if you are walking to the North End or the waterfront after dark, use the lit main streets rather than cutting through the harbourfront car parks.

How to Do an Evening Here Well

  1. Arrive at 18:00. The lunch crush is over, the square still has daylight in summer, and every counter is open.
  2. Eat at the counters first. One $8–15 item each, or split a chowder bread bowl. This is the food the market is actually for; the food guide has the must-eats and the full price bands.
  3. Walk the square while the performers work. Their published window closes at 21:00 with the market.
  4. Move to a pub or restaurant around 20:30 if you want the evening to continue, and confirm the venue’s own closing time when you arrive.
  5. Or head for the North End for the second half of the evening — a 10–12 minute walk, and the district where Bostonians actually eat late.

If you are still deciding how Quincy Market relates to the rest of the site — what to eat, where the counters are, what to skip — the restaurants and eateries directory and the Quincy Market vs Faneuil Hall comparison cover the two questions people ask most. Winter visitors should also check whether Boston’s restaurant weeks are running during their stay: the market’s own events calendar has listed Dine Out Boston, the city’s fixed-price restaurant promotion, in February and August.

Quick Answers

What time does Quincy Market close?

9:00 p.m. Monday to Saturday and 7:00 p.m. on Sunday for the marketplace, its shops and the pushcarts. The food colonnade closes earlier than the complex on Sundays — the market’s own visitor information lists the food-hall stalls at noon–18:00.

Is Quincy Market open at night?

Yes, until closing — and the last two hours are the most pleasant part of the day. After the market closes, the pubs and sit-down restaurants around it set their own hours, so the evening does not have to end when the hall does.

Where do you eat at night at Quincy Market?

At the 26 food-colonnade counters if you are there before closing, where a substantial item typically costs $8–15; or in the four full-service restaurants the operator lists — Ned Devine’s Irish Pub and The Salty Dog in the Quincy Market Building, Margaritaville in the North Market Building and Sugar Factory in the South Market Building — where mains start around $19.

Is there anything free to do at night here?

The street performers on the cobblestone square, who work daily from 11:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m., and the square itself.

Is Quincy Market safe at night?

It is a busy, well-lit, high-footfall tourist area while the market and its performers are operating, and hotels, transport and restaurants are all within a few minutes of the square. Normal city precautions apply in the crowded colonnade, and it is worth sticking to the lit main streets if you walk on to the waterfront after dark.
