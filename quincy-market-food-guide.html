Quincy Market Food Guide 2027 — Prices and What to Eat

Last checked: October 2026. Prices below are indicative 2026–27 bands assembled from published 2026 price surveys and vendor menus, not fixed menu prices; where a vendor’s own published menu is the source, it is named. Counters re-price with the season, so confirm at the counter before you order.

Quincy Market is the 27,000 sq ft market hall inside Faneuil Hall Marketplace in downtown Boston, opened in 1826 under Mayor Josiah Quincy and free to enter (see the history of Quincy Market). It is the best place in the city centre to eat well for under $20 at the counters: you walk between the counters in a single building, order at the till, and sit wherever you find space.

This guide answers the two questions people arrive with — what should I eat, and what will it cost? It covers the price bands, the four Boston dishes worth ordering, the vendor line-up checked against the operator's own tenant directory in October 2026, the status of the $18.26 bicentennial specials, and the timing and money tips that make the difference between a good lunch and an hour queueing.

If you only do one thing: order a clam chowder sourdough bread bowl at Boston Chowda and split it. It is the best-value order in the building — the vendor’s published menu lists the sourdough bread bowl at $7.49 — and the fastest, most reliable introduction to the food hall.

What a Meal Costs — 2026–27 Price Bands

Quincy Market splits into two eating experiences: the ground-floor food-hall counters, where you order and carry your own tray, and the sit-down restaurants and pubs around the perimeter. Here is what each costs per person.

Where you eat Typical price per person What that buys
Food-hall counter $8–15 One substantial item — chowder in a bread bowl, a sandwich, a gyro, a pasta bowl, or a slice with a drink. It does not cover the seafood or poke counters: lobster rolls and poke bowls sit above this band.
Sit-down restaurant or pub $18–33 A plated main with table service — seafood, Irish pub fare, Mexican, pizza. Add a drink and budget about $30–45.
Dessert, coffee or snack $4–9 Ice cream, gelato, a bakery item, a cookie, bubble tea or a coffee.
Cheapest single item Under $10 A single item at the sausage and street-food counters comes in under $10 — a hot dog with fries and a drink is about $11–13.

One caveat, and it matters: the operator does not publish stall-by-stall pricing, and the counters re-price with the season. Every figure in this guide is an indicative band assembled from published 2026 price surveys and vendor menus, not a fixed menu price, and is marked as typical rather than fixed. Use it to decide what a meal will roughly cost, then confirm on the board at the counter.

Bottom line: budget $15–18 per person at the food-hall counters and you will eat very well — an item from the $8–15 band, a drink and a dessert. The sit-down restaurants are a different tier: mains run $18–33, or about $30–45 with a drink. That gap is why the hall stays busy at lunch.

The Must-Eat List — Four Boston Dishes

Walk the colonnade for ten minutes and you will see the same four orders repeated by people who know the market. Order these and you have done Quincy Market properly. If you want the longer background on each dish, our best food at Quincy Market page covers the classic favourites; the full counter-by-counter directory is on the restaurants and eateries page.

1. Clam chowder — Boston Chowda Co.

Boston clam chowder is a cream-based soup of clams, diced potato and onion. It is thicker than the clear Rhode Island version and has nothing in common with red Manhattan chowder. Boston Chowda Co., in the Quincy Market Food Colonnade, is the stall most visitors come for; the vendor’s published menu lists a chowder in a sourdough bread bowl at $7.49, the value end of the food-hall counter band. Order it with a lobster roll and you have the classic Quincy Market lunch.

2. Lobster roll — Wicked Lobsta or Boston & Maine

A Maine-style lobster roll is chilled lobster meat in a grilled, split-top bun, dressed lightly instead of loaded with mayonnaise. Two colonnade counters specialise in them: Wicked Lobsta and Boston & Maine. Expect roughly $22–40 depending on the counter, with Wicked Lobsta at the top of that range (about $33–40) — well above the food-hall counter band, and the one order worth budgeting for. If you would rather spend less, Boston & Maine's chowder bread bowl is the value pick.

3. Boston cream pie

Despite the name, Boston cream pie is a cake: two layers of sponge filled with vanilla custard and finished with a chocolate glaze. Look for it at the bakery and dessert counters in the colonnade rather than assuming one fixed stall — the dessert line-up rotates — and expect about $5–9.

4. Baked beans and brown bread

Boston brown bread is a steamed, lightly sweet bread made with molasses and raisins. It is traditionally cooked in a can, which is why it comes out round, and it is served alongside baked beans. This is a market-wide New England speciality rather than one stall's signature, so ask at the traditional New England and bakery counters; expect about $7–11 for the plate.

Pro tip: most visitors split one chowder bread bowl between two and add a dessert. That holds the per-person bill near $10–12 and leaves room for a second stop.

The $18.26 Bicentennial Specials — Status

Quincy Market opened in 1826, and for its 200th year the Faneuil Hall Marketplace ran "The 1826 Special" — a collection of items priced at exactly $18.26 across selected vendors. It launched on 26 August 2026 and was published to run through the end of the year, that is to 31 December 2026.

Fourteen vendors took part: Bakey, North End Bakery, Boston & Maine, Wicked Lobsta, Boston Chowda, Berry Twist, The Monkey Bar, Ben & Jerry's, Vivi Bubble Tea, Wicked Dog, Sloomoo, Boston Logos, Putt Across America and Co-Operatives.

Do the specials extend into 2027? As of this guide's October 2026 check, no. The only window the operator published ends on 31 December 2026 and no 2027 extension has been announced. If you are reading this in 2027, treat the $18.26 offers as a 2026 event and rely on the regular price bands above; check faneuilhallmarketplace.com before you travel in case the promotion is re-run.

Worth knowing: before you plan a trip around the $18.26 deal, only two of the counters in this guide's vendor line-up — Bakey and Boston Chowda — were part of it. The rest of the market charged its regular prices throughout.

Practical Tips — Timing, Hours and Money

Best and worst times to go

  • Best: weekday mornings before 11:00, weekday evenings after 19:00, and Sunday before the lunch rush. The queues are short and you will find a seat.
  • Worst: 11:00–14:00, and worst of all on Saturdays and any summer weekday, when the hall is shoulder-to-shoulder and the popular counters (Boston Chowda, Wicked Lobsta) have real waits.
  • Different peaks: the counter-service hall and the sit-down rooms peak at different times — if you want table service, eat before noon or after 14:00 rather than queueing in the crush.

Opening hours — and the Sunday catch

The Faneuil Hall Marketplace complex is open Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00. The Quincy Market food hall runs its own, earlier schedule: the operator's own visitor information lists the food-hall stalls as open noon–18:00 on Sunday, and individual counters vary. Do not plan a late Sunday afternoon food-hall trip on the strength of the marketplace hours alone — confirm the day's sign at the entrance. Full hours and transport are on the useful information page.

Money-saving tips

  • Share a chowder bread bowl. At $7.49 on the vendor’s published menu it is the best value in the building, and it splits easily between two people.
  • Eat at the bottom of the band. The Dog House, West End Strollers and KongDog all sell a single item for under $10.
  • Skip the bottled soft drinks. The colonnade marks them up; water with a counter meal is cheaper.
  • Only pay for the lobster roll if it is the point of the trip. Boston & Maine's chowder bread bowl is a better value per dollar if you are watching the bill.

What to skip

  • Do not arrive at peak lunch and expect to linger. The same item costs the same, but the experience of eating standing up in a midday crowd is not worth it.
  • Do not treat the marketplace hours as the food-hall hours. They are different, especially on Sunday.
  • Do not overlook the outside. The street performers on the cobblestones are part of the market, not a nuisance — budget time to watch.

Quick Answers

Is Quincy Market free to enter?

Yes. There is no ticket and no entry fee for Quincy Market or the wider Faneuil Hall Marketplace — you pay only for what you eat and buy.

Is the food hall indoors?

Yes. The counters sit inside the 1826 market building, so the food hall works in any weather. The pushcart vendors and seating outside are open-air.

Do I need to book?

No for the food-hall counters — they are walk-up. For the sit-down restaurants, eat outside 12:00–14:00 or book ahead at peak times.
