Quincy Market Complete Food Guide with Price List 2027

Last updated: October 2026. Prices shown are the typical 2026–27 range for the Quincy Market food hall and the surrounding Faneuil Hall Marketplace. Individual stall prices change with the season — always confirm at the counter before ordering.

Quincy Market is the 27,000 sq ft historic food hall inside Faneuil Hall Marketplace, opened in 1826 by Mayor Josiah Quincy and free to enter. It is the single best place in downtown Boston to eat like a local on a budget: a full meal costs well under $20, and you can walk between 35+ stalls in a single building.

This guide covers what things actually cost in 2027, the four dishes you should not skip, the current vendor lineup (verified against the official Faneuil Hall Marketplace tenant list), the status of the 2026 bicentennial $18.26 specials, and the practical tips that save you from the worst crowds. For the rest of the city's markets — the other food halls, the farmers markets and the flea and night markets — see our Boston food and markets guide.

Price Bands at a Glance

Quincy Market splits into two eating experiences — the ground-floor food hall stalls, where you order at a counter, and the sit-down restaurants and pubs around the perimeter. Here is what each costs per person in 2026–27:

Bottom line: Budget $15–18 per person for a Quincy Market meal and you will eat very well. That is the price of a single entrée in most sit-down Boston restaurants one block away — which is exactly why the food hall stays packed at peak hours.

The Must-Eat List — Four Dishes, No Regrets

Walk the colonnade long enough and you will see the same four orders repeated by people who know Boston. Order these and you have done Quincy Market correctly.

1. Clam Chowder — Boston Chowda Co.

Boston clam chowder is a cream-based soup of clams, diced potatoes and onion, thickened with a flour-based roux. It is thicker than the clear Rhode Island version and nothing like red Manhattan chowder. Crushed crackers are traditionally crumbled on top as a garnish, but they are not the thickener. Boston Chowda Co. — a two-time chowder contest winner — serves it in a sourdough bread bowl for roughly $10–14 in the Quincy Market Food Colonnade. Order it with a lobster roll and you have the classic Quincy Market lunch.

2. Lobster Roll — Wicked Lobsta or Boston & Maine Fish Company

A Maine-style lobster roll is chilled lobster meat dressed lightly — no mayo-heavy filler, just claw and knuckle meat in a grilled split-top bun. At Quincy Market, Wicked Lobsta (4.5★) and Boston & Maine Fish Company both serve them in the Food Colonnade for about $16–20. If you want the cheaper option, Boston & Maine's clam chowder bread bowl + soft drink combo runs near the low end of the band.

3. Boston Cream Pie

Despite the name, Boston cream pie is a cake: two layers of sponge with vanilla custard or cream filling and a chocolate glaze on top. It was invented at the Parker House Hotel in 1856 (originally called "Parker House Chocolate Cream Pie"). Cream pies and custard slices appear at several dessert stalls around the marketplace — check the current day's offerings at the counter rather than assuming a specific stall, and expect roughly $5–9. For a cold alternative, Joey's Gelateria (4.6★) serves chocolate-topped gelato.

4. Baked Beans & Brown Bread

Boston brown bread is not the standard loaf. It is a slightly sweet steamed bread, sweetened with molasses or golden syrup and studded with raisins — traditionally steamed in a coffee can, which is why it is round. It is served alongside homemade baked beans, and together they are the city's oldest comfort food. This duo is a market-wide New England classic, not tied to a single stall: look for it among the traditional New England stalls in the Food Colonnade, and confirm the serving stall at the counter on the day (about $7–11 for the plate).

Pro tip: Most visitors split one chowder bread bowl between two people and add a dessert. That keeps the per-person bill around $10–12 and leaves room for a second stop.

The $18.26 Bicentennial Specials — Status for 2027

Quincy Market turned 200 on August 26, 2026, and the Faneuil Hall Marketplace launched "The 1826 Special" — a collection of themed items priced at exactly $18.26 to mark the 1826 opening year. Participating vendors included Bakey (sandwich, cookie and latte combo), North End Bakery (calzone, chips and a drink), Boston & Maine (clam chowder bread bowl and a medium soft drink), Wicked Lobsta (shrimp scampi pasta with lemonade), Boston Chowda (clam chowder bread bowl and crab cakes), The Berry Twist (acai bowl with five toppings), The Monkey Bar (any two smoothies), Ben & Jerry's (a four-flavor bicentennial ice cream flight with four toppings), and Vivi Bubble Tea (medium bubble tea, fries and popcorn chicken), plus shopping offers from Wicked Dog, Sloomoo, Boston Logos, Putt Across America and Co-Operatives.

Are they still on in 2027? The official Faneuil Hall Marketplace event page now lists the bicentennial as a past event (dated Wednesday, August 26, 2026), and the PR Newswire release confirmed the $18.26 offers ran "through the end of the year" — i.e. through December 2026. As of early 2027 the specials are not currently running, and no extension has been announced. Check faneuilhallmarketplace.com before visiting if the $18.26 deal is the reason for your trip; the regular menu prices in this guide apply in the meantime.

Heads-up: This page was written in October 2026 and reflects prices and vendors verified at that date. The bicentennial specials were always intended to end in December 2026 — if you are reading this in 2027, treat that section as history and rely on the price bands above.

Practical Tips — When to Go, How to Save, What to Avoid

Best and worst times to visit

  • Best: Weekday mornings before 11:00, weekday dinners after 19:00, and Sunday before the lunch rush. The stalls are quiet, the queues are short, and you can actually find a seat.
  • Worst: Lunch 11:00–14:00 on Saturdays and any weekday in summer. This is when the food hall is shoulder-to-shoulder and the popular stalls (Boston Chowda, Wicked Lobsta) have real waits.
  • Sunday note: The marketplace opens at 11:00 on Sunday and the food hall follows — plan an early lunch or a late afternoon visit rather than the midday crush.

Money-saving tips

  • Share a chowder bread bowl. At $10–14 it is the single best value in the building, and it is easily split between two people.
  • Hit the casual stalls first. The Dog House sausages ($7–11), West End Strollers ($7–11) and KongDog ($6–10) deliver a full meal at the low end of the band.
  • Drink what's on tap. Bottled water and soda at the colonnade are marked up — the pubs and beer gardens have better prices if you are drinking anyway.
  • Sit-down deals under $10. Several pubs and casual restaurants in the North Market Building run lunch menus with entrées under $10 — check the current day's board at the entrance rather than assuming a specific stall, since menus rotate.
  • Skip the premium lobster roll if you are on a budget. Boston & Maine's clam chowder bread bowl + soft drink combo sits near the low end and is a better value per dollar.

What to avoid

  • Peak-lunch crowds are real. The same item costs the same, but the experience of eating standing up in a crowd is not worth the trip during the midday crush.
  • Street performers are part of the draw, not a nuisance. The sword swallowers and jugglers on the cobblestones are a genuine Boston attraction — budget extra time if you want to watch.
  • Watch your bag. The food hall is crowded and the pickpocket risk is low but not zero; keep wallets in front pockets.
Quick summary: Budget $15–18 per person for a Quincy Market meal. Order clam chowder in a bread bowl, split it if you can, add a lobster roll or a slice of Boston cream pie, and arrive before 11:00 or after 19:00 to skip the worst crowds. Free admission, 3 MBTA T stops within a block, and the whole thing sits on the Freedom Trail — it is one of the best-value meals in downtown Boston.
