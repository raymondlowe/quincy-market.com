Quincy Market Bicentennial — 200 Years, and What Is Still On

Last checked: 13 October 2026. The bicentennial programme below was read from the operator's own press releases and event pages in October 2026 — the $18.26 offer is a dated 2026 event with a published end date, so re-check before you travel. Admission to the market is free and has been since 1826.

Quincy Market opened to the public on 26 August 1826, which makes 2026 its 200th year. The operator, Faneuil Hall Marketplace, marked the anniversary with “The 1826 Special” — a collection of offerings priced at exactly $18.26, the year of the building, from fourteen tenants — alongside restoration work and a run of new openings across the marketplace.

This page answers what the bicentennial actually consisted of, which parts are still running, and what the 200th year changed about the market: the history behind the date, the full list of $18.26 offers, the physical improvements completed in 2026, and the honest position on 2027 — where nothing bicentennial has been announced.

The one-line answer: the bicentennial is a 2026 event. The $18.26 “1826 Special” launched on 26 August 2026 and is published to run through 31 December 2026 — no 2027 extension has been announced. The 200th anniversary itself falls on the market's 26 August opening date; the market is open year-round, free to enter, and unchanged in price apart from the specials.

Why 2026 Is the Bicentennial — the Dates That Matter

The bicentennial counts from the building's opening, not from the earlier Faneuil Hall. Faneuil Hall itself dates from 1742; Quincy Market is the market hall Boston built beside it when the 1742 market floor could no longer feed the city. The market's own history page tells the story plainly: in May 1823 the newly elected mayor, Josiah Quincy, found the 7,600 sq ft market under his Faneuil Hall office overwhelmed, with wooden sheds spilling around the building.

  • 27 April 1825 — Mayor Quincy laid the cornerstone.
  • August 1826 — the building was completed to the design of Alexander Parris, the Greek Revival architect and engineer.
  • 26 August 1826 — the market house opened to the public. That is the date the 2026 bicentennial marks.
  • 1827 — the North Market and South Market buildings were completed the following year, giving the site the three-building shape it still has.

The naming is one of the better stories in Boston and it is worth knowing before you visit. Quincy wanted the building named for Peter Faneuil, who had given the city the earlier hall, and refused the honour at his own public speech. The City Council named it for Quincy anyway — and reserved his name instead for the ornate central chamber on the second floor, which became Quincy Hall. The formal name stayed Faneuil Hall Market for generations, and the gilded “Quincy Market” signs were only added to both Greek Revival porticoes in 1989.

Two further figures are useful for anyone reading up on the anniversary: the building is a National Historic Landmark and a Boston Landmark, and the 1976 restoration that turned the site into what is generally credited as the first “festival marketplace” in the United States is the reason Quincy Market looks the way it does today rather than as a wholesale produce hall.

Does the $18.26 special run into 2027?

On the evidence available as of October 2026: no. The only published window ends on 31 December 2026, and no extension into 2027 has been announced by the operator. If you are reading this in 2027, treat the $18.26 offer as a 2026 bicentennial event and budget from the regular price bands instead — a food-hall counter item typically runs $8–15, and a sit-down main $18–33. What Else the 200th Year Changed in the Market

The bicentennial year was not only a price promotion. The operator used it to push through physical and commercial changes that visitors in 2027 will notice.

Change in 2026 What it means on the ground
Restoration, phase one A multi-phase improvement plan run by Boston contractor Commodore Builders. The first step was completed in summer 2026: granite pavers reset across the promenades, the historic building exteriors and the glass sheds cleaned, and deferred maintenance repairs addressed — work the operator describes as left over from previous ownership.
Occupancy up Ground-floor retail occupancy rose from 76% in 2024 to 85% in 2026, with seasonal tenants pushing it higher in summer. Fewer empty units means more of the North and South Market buildings are worth walking.
New food and drink Tree House Brewing Company ran a summer pop-up with outdoor seating in the West End — seasonal, so confirm off-season status before planning around it. Smashed by BRED, The Maharaja, Azul Mexican Kitchen and Cognitive Surplus were announced as new main-hall offerings, in collaboration with leasing agency Graffito.
New shops and experiences Mona Sweets, Captain Candy and Iris Galerie opened during 2026 and are described by the operator as recent openings.
One caveat we will not paper over: the operator's press releases and its own tenant directory do not agree. Smashed by BRED, The Maharaja and Azul Mexican Kitchen are named in the operator's 2026 announcements but did not appear on the current-tenants directory when the lineup was checked in October 2026 — and the directory is demonstrably incomplete, because it also omits three tenants the operator itself calls recent openings. Treat the four names above as announced, with opening dates to confirm, not as counters you can count on finding. Check at the entrance on the day.

200 Years in One Table

If you are here for the anniversary rather than the offers, this is the compressed history that the bicentennial is counting.

Year What happened
1823 Mayor Josiah Quincy takes office, finds the 7,600 sq ft market floor at Faneuil Hall overwhelmed, and pushes a new market hall through a city committee.
1825 Cornerstone laid on 27 April by Mayor Quincy. Land assembly was complicated enough that one estate — Nathan Spear’s — explains why South Market Street is wider than North Market Street.
1826 Alexander Parris’s Greek Revival market hall completed in August and opened to the public on 26 August. The bicentennial date.
1827 North Market and South Market buildings completed, creating the three-building marketplace you walk through today.
1850s The market hits capacity, and proposals to add storeys and widen the side markets are rejected to preserve Parris’s design. Trade shifts decisively toward wholesale.
1976 The restoration that created the festival marketplace — the model credited as the first of its kind in the United States — and the year America’s first retail specialty carts programme began at Faneuil Hall Marketplace. Today more than 40 local vendors run those carts.
1989 Gilded “Quincy Market” signs added to both porticoes to commemorate Mayor Quincy’s role.
2026 Bicentennial: the $18.26 “1826 Special”, phase-one restoration, occupancy up to 85%, and a run of new openings. Over 80 businesses now trade across a marketplace the operator says welcomes more than 18 million visitors a year.

Visiting in the Bicentennial Year — the Practical Bits

  • Admission is free. There is no ticket and no entry fee for Quincy Market or the wider Faneuil Hall Marketplace, in the bicentennial year or any other. You pay only for what you eat and buy.
  • Hours: the marketplace runs Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00. The Quincy Market food hall closes earlier than the complex on Sunday — the market's own visitor information lists the food-hall stalls at noon–18:00 — so do not plan a late Sunday food trip on the complex hours alone.
  • Getting there: three MBTA stops sit within a block (Green Line Haymarket and Government Center, Blue Line Aquarium, Orange Line State/Haymarket), and the market is a marked stop on the Freedom Trail. Full directions, parking rates and the Logan Airport route are on our how to get to Quincy Market page.
  • Eating: a food-hall counter item typically costs $8–15 and a sit-down main $18–33; the current price bands, per-vendor detail and money tips are in the food guide with price list. The full stall directory is on the restaurants and eateries page.
  • Evening: the street performers work the cobblestone square daily from 11:00 to 21:00, which makes the last two hours before closing the best free show in the marketplace. More in Quincy Market at night.

What to Expect in 2027

Being straight about this matters more than filling the section. As of the October 2026 check:

  • No 2027 bicentennial programme has been announced. The 200th anniversary year is 2026, and the operator's published campaign runs to 31 December 2026.
  • The $18.26 offer is expected to lapse with the calendar year, unless the operator re-runs it — which it has left itself room to do with the phrase “unless otherwise specified online”.
  • Restoration continues. The 2026 work was described by the operator as the first step of a multi-phase plan, so expect further works around the promenades. Worth checking the operator's site for scaffolding or closed sections if you are planning a photography-heavy visit.
  • The tenant line-up will keep moving. Announced-but-unlisted names from 2026 should either appear on the directory or quietly drop out; the directory is the source to trust on the day.

The market's own events calendar, linked from faneuilhallmarketplace.com, is the place to confirm anything dated before you travel in 2027 — the bicentennial page itself now carries a “past event” flag, which is the honest signal that the campaign, not the market, is what ended.

Quick Answers

Was Quincy Market named after Mayor Josiah Quincy?

Yes — despite Quincy asking that the building honour Peter Faneuil instead, and refusing the honour at his own speech. The City Council named the market for Quincy and gave his name to the second-floor central chamber, Quincy Hall. The market's formal name remained Faneuil Hall Market for well over a century.

Is the $18.26 special still on?

It ran from 26 August 2026 to a published end of 31 December 2026. If you are reading this in 2027, assume it has ended and check the operator's site for a re-run; the regular price bands in our food guide apply either way.

Is there a bicentennial event in 2027?

None announced as of October 2026. The 200th year is 2026. What 2027 does hold is the market's normal annual rhythm — the late-November tree lighting, the Christmas season and the summer street-performer programme, plus whatever the next phase of the restoration brings.

How does the bicentennial relate to Faneuil Hall?

Two different buildings, two different birthdays. Faneuil Hall dates from 1742; Quincy Market is the 1826 market hall built beside it as an expansion. If that distinction still feels slippery, our Quincy Market vs Faneuil Hall comparison settles it in one line.
