Christmas at Quincy Market — Tree Lighting, Holiday Markets & Hours

Last checked: 13 October 2026. The tree-lighting date is announced by the operator each autumn, so no ceremony date is fixed for the coming season yet. This page documents exactly what the operator has published and what the previous two seasons did, so you know what to expect and when to check back. Nothing below is a 2027 promise.

Christmas at Quincy Market is built on one free public event and a lot of ordinary market hours. Each year the operator plants a large spruce in the West End, between historic Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market, and lights it at a ceremony in late November. The tree then stands for the whole season, the marketplace keeps its normal schedule with the operator’s standing caveat that holiday, restaurant and retail hours may vary, and the holiday shopping — a year-round Christmas shop in the North Market Building, the pushcarts under the canopy, and an announced Christmas Market whose details are still to be published — runs alongside it.

The short answer for planning: the ceremony is free and open to the public, runs on a late-November afternoon into early evening, and the tree is lit at a fixed time — 6:00 p.m. sharp in 2025. The market itself needs no booking, no ticket and no planning: it is open every day of the season except where the operator states otherwise. If you only want the market without the ceremony crowds, come on a weekday morning in December.

The Tree Lighting — What Actually Happens

The operator runs an Annual Tree Lighting Celebration. The pattern of the last two seasons is the most reliable guide to what the next one will look like:

Season Date and time What the operator ran
2025 Saturday 22 November 2025, 2:00–19:00, tree lit at 6:00 p.m. sharp A 50 ft tree in the West End between Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market; Santa and Miss Boston 2025; face painting; festive treats; and live performances from Commonwealth Shakespeare Co., North End Music & Performing Arts Center, BoSoma Dance Company, Boston Children’s Chorus, City Ballet of Boston and The Femmes. The charity partner was Make-A-Wish Massachusetts and Rhode Island, and a Make-A-Wish child helped switch on the lights. Free, open to the public, no ticket.
2024 Tuesday 26 November 2024 An afternoon of performances, guest appearances and festive treats, hosted by 7NEWS anchor Amaka Ubaka — the same emcee as 2025.

Two details are worth planning around. First, the ceremony is an afternoon-long event with a fixed lighting moment: in 2025 the entertainment started at 2:00 p.m. and the switch was thrown at 6:00 p.m. Arriving at 6:00 for the tree means missing the performances; arriving at 2:00 means standing in the cold for four hours. The sweet spot in 2025 terms was roughly 4:30–5:30 p.m. Second, the tree is sourced from a partner farm — the 50 ft 2025 tree came from Michigan, and the farm has supplied the marketplace for over a decade, so the size and shape has been consistent rather than a surprise each year.

The tree then stays up for the holiday season rather than being removed after the ceremony. If you miss the lighting night entirely, the tree is still there, lit, throughout December.

Is the Blink! light show running?

If you have read about Quincy Market at Christmas, you have probably read about Blink! — a light-and-sound show in which the trees on the market's open spaces flash in patterns, historically using more than 350,000 LED lights, choreographed to music. The market's open spaces have carried Christmas trees since 1989, and the trees have hosted Blink! since 2012.

The honest position: Blink! has not run every year, and no year should be assumed. It was suspended in 2020, was not held in 2021 or 2022, and the 2025 tree lighting was billed as the show’s kick-off. Treat it as a bonus rather than the reason for the trip, and check the operator's events calendar in the week before you travel — the tree and the market are the constants, the show is not.

The Christmas Market and Holiday Shopping

The operator has announced that a Christmas Market is coming to Faneuil Hall Marketplace and has been recruiting operators for it — the call for Christmas operators has been live on the operator's homepage. Dates, opening hours, stall count and location have not been published at the time of this check, so any blog quoting “60+ vendors” or a fixed December run is guessing.

What is certain is that Quincy Market is a genuinely useful Christmas shopping base whatever the pop-up programme turns out to be, because the good stuff is indoors:

  • Christmas in Boston — a year-round Christmas shop in the North Market Building selling ornaments, personalised decorations and gifts. Published hours are Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00, with the operator’s standing holiday caveat.
  • Holiday makers’ markets inside the building. In recent Decembers the second floor of Quincy Market has hosted multi-week indoor markets from independent local sellers — the Boston Women’s Market, for example, ran a December market with more than 40 women-owned businesses. These are year-by-year arrangements, so check the operator’s calendar rather than assuming.
  • Pushcarts and the 40+ local vendors. The specialty carts programme that began here in 1976 still runs; in December it is where the small souvenirs and stocking-fillers are.
  • The food colonnade. The single most weather-proof Christmas lunch in downtown Boston: 26 counter vendors under one roof, a chowder bread bowl from Boston Chowda for $7.49 at the value end, and no need to queue outside. Prices and per-stall detail are in the food guide.

Hours Over the Holidays

The marketplace keeps its standard schedule through the season, with the operator explicitly reserving the right to vary it:

Day Faneuil Hall Marketplace What to know
Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 The published complex hours. Individual shops, restaurants and pubs may open later or close earlier — the operator's own footnote reads “Holiday, restaurant, and retail hours may vary”.
Sunday 11:00–19:00 The Quincy Market food hall closes earlier on Sunday than the complex does: the market’s own visitor information lists the food-hall stalls at noon–18:00. Do not plan a late Sunday-afternoon food trip around the complex hours.
Christmas Eve & New Year’s Eve Not published as a fixed schedule Hours for these days are posted by the operator nearer the time rather than published as a standing schedule. Check the operator’s events calendar and the signs at the entrances instead of assuming the standard schedule.
Christmas Day Not published as a fixed schedule Check before you travel. Haymarket — the open-air produce market on the doorstep, which runs Fridays and Saturdays year-round — does not trade on 25 December or 1 January.
Admission is free, as always. There is no ticket, no entry fee and no seasonal surcharge for the market, the tree or the ceremony. You pay only for what you eat and buy — and the free showing is the street performers on the cobblestones, who work daily from 11:00 to 21:00, weather permitting.

Other Christmas Things Within Walking Distance

Quincy Market is the free, indoor, no-planning part of a Boston Christmas. If you are building a day around it, two other fixtures sit close enough to pair with it:

  • Boston Common and the city’s official Christmas tree — roughly 1.2 km away and about five minutes by car, or a 15–20 minute walk up through Downtown Crossing. Boston’s official tree is lit in early December, separately from the Quincy Market ceremony, so the two do not clash.
  • First Night Boston on 31 December — the city’s New Year’s Eve arts festival, which has been listed on the market’s own events calendar in past seasons.

There is also a Chanukah element to the season at this address: a Grand Menorah Lighting at Faneuil Hall and Quincy Market, run by Chabad of Downtown Boston, has featured in recent holiday round-ups alongside the city’s other menorah lightings. Dates move with the Hebrew calendar, so check the operator’s calendar for the year you are travelling.

Practical Advice for a December Visit

  • Book the hotel before you book anything else. December weekends are among the busiest of the year in downtown Boston. Hotels within a two-to-five-minute walk of the market exist — The Bostonian, Canopy by Hilton, Harborside Inn — and the price bands move daily, so the earlier you commit the better.
  • Take the T. Three stops are within a block (Green Line Haymarket and Government Center, Blue Line Aquarium, Orange Line State/Haymarket). In snow and slush that beats circling for a space. Full routes are on our how to get here page.
  • If you do drive, the official garage never closes. 75 State Street Garage, entrance at 5 Broad Street, is open 24/7; Dock Square at 20 Clinton Street is usually cheaper at roughly $29 on a weekday and $25 at the weekend. Rates move — confirm at the board.
  • Come early or come late, not at lunch. The colonnade is shoulder-to-shoulder from 11:00 to 14:00 and worst on December weekends. Arriving at opening (10:00) or after 19:00 gets you seating and short queues.
  • Rain and snow do not cancel the market. The counters are inside the 1826 building, so the food hall works in any weather. The pushcarts, the tree and the performers are outdoors.
  • Eat inside, not outside. The counters and the seating around the central rotunda are all under the 1826 building’s roof, so a cold day is an eat-inside day rather than a change of plan. The pushcarts, the tree and the performers are the parts that are outdoors.

When the Dates Are Announced

Because the ceremony moves every year — 22 November in 2025, 26 November in 2024 — the date is published by the operator in the autumn rather than fixed in advance. The reliable source is the operator’s own events calendar at faneuilhallmarketplace.com, which is also where the Christmas Market details will appear when they are confirmed.

For the market itself, there is nothing to announce and nothing to wait for: it opens every day of the season on the hours above, admission is free, and the food hall is warm. Quick Answers

When is the Quincy Market Christmas tree lighting?

Late November, on a date the operator announces each autumn. Recent editions were Tuesday 26 November 2024 and Saturday 22 November 2025, with the 2025 lighting at 6:00 p.m. sharp and entertainment from 2:00 p.m.

Is it free?

Yes. The tree lighting ceremony is free and open to the public, and admission to Quincy Market and Faneuil Hall Marketplace is free every day of the year.

How much does the tree cost to see?

Nothing — and it stays up through the season, so the tree can be seen for free on any December day, not only on lighting night.

Is there a Christmas market at Faneuil Hall?

The operator has announced an upcoming Christmas Market and has been recruiting operators for it. No dates or opening hours had been published at the time of this check, so confirm on the operator’s calendar before travelling for it specifically.

What are the hours over Christmas?

The standard schedule is Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00, with the Quincy Market food hall closing earlier on Sunday (noon–18:00). The operator states that holiday, restaurant and retail hours may vary, so check the day’s hours on arrival for Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and New Year’s Eve — none of which is published as a fixed schedule.
