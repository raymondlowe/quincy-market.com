Are there any Boston markets left? Are all Boston markets closed?

Last updated: October 2026. Opening times, season dates and restaurant counts change — always confirm with the operator or on the official site before you travel.

Two completely different things get called "Boston markets", and they have opposite answers. If you mean the Boston Market restaurant chain — the rotisserie chicken chain founded in Newton, Massachusetts, in 1985 — most of it has closed, but not all of it: roughly two dozen restaurants still trade in the United States, and there is no longer a single one in Massachusetts. If you mean the markets in Boston — Quincy Market, Haymarket, the Boston Public Market — they are all open, and several of them trade every week of the year.

Short answer: Yes, Boston still has markets. Quincy Market and the Faneuil Hall Marketplace, Haymarket, the Boston Public Market, the SoWa Open Market and the Copley Square Farmers Market are all trading in 2026–27. And no, not all "Boston Markets" are closed either — but the chicken chain is down to about two dozen US restaurants and has none left in its home state.

If you mean the Boston Market restaurant chain

The confusion is understandable: Boston Market the restaurant began life in Newton, Massachusetts, in 1985 as "Boston Chicken", took its present name in 1995, and is now owned by Engage Brands LLC and headquartered in Newtown Township, Pennsylvania.

How many Boston Market restaurants are left?

About two dozen nationwide. The chain peaked at more than 1,000 restaurants in the 1990s — some accounts put the peak above 1,200 — and still had around 342 company-owned locations in November 2020. It then collapsed quickly: it began 2023 with roughly 300 locations, was down to about 79 later that year, and was reported at fewer than 30 by early 2024. By 2026 the Boston Globe reported "about two dozen restaurants nationwide", and Restaurant Business has said more than 90 per cent of Boston Market locations have closed since the end of 2022. The exact figure is disputed — the chain's own website was reported in January 2026 as listing 22 locations, and Wikipedia's entry records 22 as of March 2025 — so treat anything in the region of 20 to 25 as the honest answer.

Are there any Boston Market restaurants left in Massachusetts?

No. There has been no Boston Market restaurant in Massachusetts since 2025. The last one in the state — on Park Avenue in Worcester — closed during 2025. Before that the chain had already retreated from Greater Boston: the Quincy branch at 126 Granite Street closed in November 2024, leaving Medford as the only restaurant inside Route 128, with others further out in Brockton and Framingham. The Boston Globe reports that the nearest surviving Boston Market to Massachusetts is now in Hackensack, New Jersey, and warns that the chain's website still points to shuttered Massachusetts stores.

Is the Boston Market brand gone as well?

No — which is why you may still see the name in a supermarket. Boston Market-branded frozen meals and side dishes continue to be sold nationally, made under licence by Bellisio Foods. A Boston Market chicken pot pie on a freezer shelf is not a restaurant, and the grocery brand has outlived most of the chain's dining rooms.

Why did so many Boston Market restaurants close?

The short version: a 2020 sale to Engage Brands was followed by unpaid suppliers, unpaid rent and unpaid wages, which produced more than 150 lawsuits. In August 2023 New Jersey issued a stop-work order at 27 Boston Market locations over owed back pay; in November 2023 Detroit and Connecticut locations were permanently shuttered with eviction notices for unpaid rent; and the company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in December 2023. Locations kept closing through 2024 and 2025.

If you mean markets in Boston — these are still open

Boston's markets are a mix of one historic indoor food hall, one centuries-old open-air pushcart market and several indoor and outdoor producers' markets. Every market listed in the table below is trading in 2026–27.

Market Type When it is open Where
Quincy Market
(Faneuil Hall Marketplace)		 Indoor food hall and marketplace Every day: Mon–Sat 10:00–21:00, Sun 11:00–19:00. The food hall itself closes earlier on Sundays (typically 12:00–18:00) 206 S Market St, downtown Boston
Haymarket Open-air fruit, vegetable and seafood pushcart market Fridays and Saturdays only, from dawn to dusk — there are no official hours; vendors set up from about 06:00 Blackstone, Hanover and North Streets, beside the Greenway
Boston Public Market Indoor, year-round market — everything produced or sourced in New England Every day: Sun–Tue 08:00–18:00, Wed–Sat 08:00–20:00 (individual vendors vary) 100 Hanover St, above Haymarket T station
SoWa Open Market Open-air art, craft and farmers' market Sundays 11:00–17:00, 3 May – 15 Nov 2026 (closed in winter) 500 Harrison Ave, South End
Copley Square Farmers Market Farmers' market — Boston's largest and busiest Tuesdays and Fridays 11:00–18:00, 15 May – 24 Nov 2026 227–230 Dartmouth St, Copley Square (McKim Plaza)

Two of these need a moment of explanation, because visitors mix them up constantly. Haymarket is the historic one: it is an open-air market on the streets themselves, run by the Haymarket Pushcart Association, and it is the closest thing Boston has to a permanent street market — markets have been held in this part of the city since the 1600s. The Boston Public Market is the modern one: an indoor, year-round hall beside Haymarket station, a short walk from Quincy Market, where everything on sale is produced in or sourced from New England. Both sit within a short walk of Quincy Market, so you can visit all three in one morning — Haymarket on a Friday or Saturday, the other two any day.

How long have Boston's markets been running?

Longer than the United States has existed. Markets have been held in the Haymarket area since the 1600s; the first market buildings there went up in 1734, and the indoor market at Faneuil Hall opened in 1742. Open-air trading has continued in the same few streets since the early 19th century — the Haymarket Pushcart Association traces its roots to 1820 — and the current site and trading days were fixed by a 1952 state law and a 1978 city ordinance. Quincy Market itself opened in 1826 under Mayor Josiah Quincy. That continuity is the reason the answer to "are all Boston markets closed?" is so emphatically no.

Boston markets FAQ

Are there any Boston markets left?

Yes — in both senses. Quincy Market and the Faneuil Hall Marketplace, Haymarket, the Boston Public Market, the SoWa Open Market and the Copley Square Farmers Market are all open in 2026–27. And the Boston Market restaurant chain, while much diminished, still has roughly two dozen restaurants nationwide.

Are all Boston markets closed?

No — every market named in this article is open. The Boston Market restaurant chain is a separate story: it has lost more than 90 per cent of its locations since the end of 2022, and has no restaurant left in Massachusetts, but it is not fully closed.

Which Boston markets are open in winter?

Quincy Market and the Faneuil Hall Marketplace, the Boston Public Market and Haymarket all trade year-round. The SoWa Open Market and the Copley Square Farmers Market are seasonal, running from May to November.

Are Boston's markets free to enter?

Yes. Quincy Market and the Faneuil Hall Marketplace have no ticket and no entry fee — you spend only on food and shopping. Haymarket and the city's farmers' markets are street and plaza markets, so there is nothing to book either.

When is Haymarket open?

Fridays and Saturdays only, from dawn to dusk, all year. There are no official hours: traders arrive from around 06:00 and leave when the produce is gone or the weather wins. Prices are often half what you would pay in a supermarket, and late on Saturday afternoon vendors sell off what is left.

What is the closest Boston Market restaurant to Boston?

Hackensack, New Jersey, according to the Boston Globe — there is no Boston Market restaurant left in Massachusetts at all. If you are in Boston and want rotisserie chicken, you will need an independent restaurant rather than the chain.

Is Boston Market the restaurant the same as Quincy Market?

No, and they are not related in any way. Boston Market is a chicken restaurant chain that took its name from the city where it was founded. Quincy Market is the 1826 food hall in the Faneuil Hall Marketplace. If you searched for one and landed on the other, that is why — and if it is the market you want, start with our Quincy Market guide or the full list of markets in Boston.

In one line: Boston's markets are still trading — Quincy Market, Haymarket, the Boston Public Market and the seasonal markets are all open in 2026–27. The Boston Market chicken chain did mostly close: about two dozen restaurants remain in the United States, and none in Massachusetts.
