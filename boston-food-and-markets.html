Boston Food & Markets — Where to Eat, Shop and Browse in 2027

🌐 Language: English | Boston guides in Deutsch · Nederlands · Español

Last updated: October 2026. Opening days, seasonal dates and prices below were checked against the official market websites in October 2026 and change year to year — confirm before you travel.

Boston eats out of markets. The city's food is easiest to understand as a ring of halls and open-air pitches around the historic centre: the 1826 food hall at Quincy Market, the year-round Boston Public Market one block away, the big Fenway and North Station food halls, and a weekly rhythm of farmers, flea and night markets that runs from spring to late autumn.

This page answers the three questions people actually search: where to eat in Boston on a market budget, which farmers, flea and night markets are open and on what days, and where to shop near Quincy Market. Every opening day and time below is sourced — the dates given are the 2026 season, because that is what the official market websites currently publish; the recurring pattern is what matters for a 2027 visit.

Boston markets at a glance: Quincy Market — open daily, free entry, over 25 eateries · Boston Public Market — open daily, over 30 New England producers · Haymarket — Fridays and Saturdays all year, Boston's oldest outdoor market (1820) · SoWa Open Market — Sundays, May–November, free · SoWa Vintage Market — every Sunday, year round, free · a night market roughly every month in summer.

Where to eat in Boston — the city's food halls

Boston has five food halls worth planning a meal around. All five are indoors, all five work the same way — order at separate counters, pay at each one, seat yourself — and that is why locals treat them as the default answer to a rainy day in Boston.

Quincy Market — the historic one, and the best value

Quincy Market is the 27,000 sq ft food hall inside Faneuil Hall Marketplace, opened in 1826 under Mayor Josiah Quincy. Entry is free — there is no ticket and no gate. Inside, more than 25 eateries ("Best of Boston") sit alongside the colonnade stalls, and the wider marketplace holds over 40 specialty carts and shops. Food-hall meals run roughly $8–15 per person at the counter stalls and about $10–18 for a sit-down restaurant or pub meal; desserts, coffee and snacks are $4–9. The full stall-by-stall price list is in our Quincy Market food guide with prices, and the dish list is on the Best Food page. The marketplace is open Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00; the Quincy Market food hall itself closes earlier on Sundays, typically around 12:00–18:00, so plan Sunday lunch rather than a late Sunday afternoon visit and check the hours at the entrance.

Boston Public Market — New England producers under one roof

At 100 Hanover Street, directly above the Haymarket T station, Boston Public Market is an indoor, year-round marketplace with over 30 New England artisans and food producers selling fresh produce, meat, eggs, dairy, seafood, baked goods, prepared meals and crafts. It is free to enter and open daily: Sunday–Tuesday 08:00–18:00, Wednesday–Saturday 08:00–20:00, with individual vendor hours varying. Because it sits on top of Haymarket and next to Quincy Market, it is the obvious second stop on a downtown food walk. Parking is validated by any vendor: $3 for up to three hours at the Haymarket Garage, entrance 136 Blackstone Street.

Time Out Market Boston — the Fenway food hall

Time Out Market Boston is at 401 Park Drive, three blocks west of Fenway Park, and brings 15 food and drink offerings under one roof — from Asian-fusion concepts to Boston BBQ, Italian and Mediterranean street food. Hours are Monday–Thursday 08:30–21:00, Friday 08:30–22:00, Saturday 09:00–22:00 and Sunday 09:00–21:00; individual kitchens may open or close slightly around those times, and the bars open at 11:30. The Green Line (D branch) stops at Fenway and the Framingham/Worcester commuter rail at Lansdowne. Note that the kitchens and bars are cashless — bring a card, or buy a market card at the welcome desk.

Hub Hall — next to TD Garden and North Station

Hub Hall, at 80 Causeway Street inside The Hub on Causeway, is the largest of the modern halls: 18 food and drink options, from ramen and tacos to burgers, lobster rolls and cannoli. It opens earlier than anything else in town because it serves commuters — Sunday 07:00–22:00, Monday–Thursday 06:00–22:30, Friday 06:00–23:00, Saturday 07:00–23:00. It is adjacent to TD Garden and North Station, so it works as a meal before or after a game.

High Street Place — downtown, closed on Sundays

High Street Place at 100 High Street is downtown Boston's food hall, with 20 local eateries and bars. Hours are Monday–Wednesday 07:00–21:00, Thursday–Friday 07:00–23:00 and Saturday 09:00–23:00 — it is closed on Sundays. Food stalls close an hour before the posted closing time; the bars run to the times above.

Farmers markets in Boston — what's open and when

Boston's farmers markets are mostly seasonal and mostly one-day-a-week, which is why "farmers market near me" is such a frustrating search in this city. Below is the actual weekly pattern for the 2026 season — the same pattern repeats each year, so a 2027 visitor can use it to pick the day.

Haymarket — Fridays and Saturdays, all year

Haymarket is Boston's oldest outdoor market, established in 1820 and operating on Blackstone Street between Hanover Street and North Street, right beside Quincy Market. It runs Friday and Saturday only, year round (the only closures are Christmas Day and New Year's Day), and it is the cheap one: pushcart vendors sell fruit, vegetables and seafood at prices that undercut the supermarkets. The market has no official hours — the official site lists Friday from 03:00 and Saturday from 04:00 until close, while in practice most vendors set up around 06:00 and trade until about 18:00. The best produce arrives early; the deepest discounts come late on Saturday afternoon. Vendors validate parking at the Parcel 7 Garage, 136 Blackstone Street: $1 for two hours, $3 for three hours.

SoWa Open Market — Sundays, May to November

SoWa Open Market is one of the largest open-air farmer and artist markets in Boston, on the pedestrian-only Thayer Street in the South End. The 2026 season ran every Sunday from 3 May to 15 November, 11:00–17:00, and admission is free. It mixes regional farmers with artists, makers and food trucks, and spills into the galleries and boutiques of the SoWa Art + Design District. Address: 500 Harrison Avenue and Thayer Street.

Boston Public Market on the Greenway — Thursdays, June to October

A satellite of the indoor Boston Public Market sets up on the Rose Kennedy Greenway on Thursdays from 14:00 to 16:00, June to October, selling Massachusetts produce, fresh seafood, artisan cheese and bread. It is a small, quick stop rather than a full market day — pair it with the Greenway's food trucks and carousel.

Copley Square Farmers Market — Tuesdays and Fridays

The Copley Square Farmers Market in Back Bay is the downtown-worker market: the 2026 season ran Tuesdays and Fridays, 11:00–18:00, from 15 May to 24 November. It is the easiest weekday farmers market to reach from Quincy Market — take the Green Line to Copley.

Chinatown Farmers Market — selected Saturdays on the Greenway

At Auntie Kay and Uncle Frank Chin Park on the Greenway, the Chinatown Farmers Market runs on selected Saturdays from 09:00 to 13:00 between July and October — the published 2026 dates were 4 and 25 July, 1 and 22 August, 5 and 26 September and 10 and 24 October. It is the market for Asian fruit and vegetables that the supermarket chains do not carry. Because the dates are patchy, check the Greenway calendar before making the trip.

Neighbourhood markets worth the detour

If you are staying outside downtown, the 2026 season also included Roslindale Village on Saturdays 09:00–13:30 (4 June – 21 November), Central Square in Cambridge on Mondays 12:00–18:00 (18 May – 23 November), Davis Square on Wednesdays 12:00–18:00 (20 May – 25 November) and East Boston on Wednesdays 15:00–18:30 (8 July – 28 October).

Flea markets and vintage shopping in Boston

Boston does not have a huge permanent flea market, but it does have a weekly vintage market, a Sunday open-air market and a five-floor antique house — enough to answer "flea market near me" properly.

SoWa Vintage Market — every Sunday, all year

The SoWa Vintage Market is the one to plan around: downstairs at 450 Harrison Avenue in the South End, it is open every Sunday from 11:00 to 16:00, year round, rain or shine, plus a First Friday edition each month from 17:00 to 21:00. Expect curated fashion, jewellery, art, furniture and objects rather than car-boot clutter. Admission is free, and it sits a minute from the Sunday SoWa Open Market, so the two combine into a full South End day.

Cambridge Antique Market — Tuesday to Sunday

Across the river, the Cambridge Antique Market at 201 Msgr. O'Brien Highway is the serious hunt: five floors, over 150 dealer spaces, and furniture, artwork, silver, glass, china, toys, books, vintage clothing and collectibles. It is open Tuesday–Sunday, 11:00–18:00 and closed on Mondays.

Flea-style browsing inside Quincy Market

If you are already at Quincy Market, the marketplace's own cart programme is the closest thing downtown to a flea market: America's first retail specialty carts programme began at Faneuil Hall Marketplace in 1976, and over 40 local vendors still sell apparel, gifts and accessories from the carts and shops. It is open the same hours as the marketplace — Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00, Sunday 11:00–19:00.

Night markets in Boston

"Night market Boston" is a slightly misleading search, because Boston has no permanent, every-night market. What it does have is a handful of recurring evening markets in the warmer months — free to enter, food-led, and each one different.

The Night Bazaar — Boston's original night market

The Night Bazaar, run by the Brighton Bazaar at the Charles River Speedway (525 Western Avenue, Brighton), bills itself as Boston's original night market and returned for its fifth summer in 2026. It runs on Thursdays and Fridays, 17:00–22:00, with free admission and around 100 independent vendors selling art, vinyl, vintage and food, alongside the Speedway's outdoor courtyard, taproom and restaurants. The 2026 editions included 25 June, 23–24 July and a season finale on 13–14 August, so expect a monthly-ish summer cadence rather than a weekly one.

Chinatown Night Market — one Saturday on the Greenway

The Chinatown Night Market takes over Auntie Kay and Uncle Frank Chin Park on the Rose Kennedy Greenway for a single evening: the published 2026 date was Saturday 19 September, 17:00–22:00, with food stands strung between Hudson and Beach Street plus dance performances and craft stalls. It is a one-night event, not a weekly market — check the Greenway's events calendar for the current season's date before you build an evening around it.

Evening markets to watch for

Beyond those two, Boston's summer calendar fills with one-off evening and neighbourhood markets — the city's 2026 summer events listing included a Boston Little Saigon Night Market in Dorchester on 18 July, and the Greenway runs food-truck festivals and artisan markets alongside its Chinatown dates. The reliable way to find what is on during your trip is the Greenway's events calendar and The Boston Calendar, both of which publish dates only a few weeks ahead.

Shopping near Quincy Market — what to buy, and where

The "shopping near me" search around Quincy Market resolves into three shopping zones, plus one tax rule worth knowing.

Faneuil Hall Marketplace and Quincy Market

This is the souvenir zone, and the most convenient one: over 40 local vendors across the carts and shops, selling apparel, gifts, accessories and Boston-themed merchandise, plus the food hall for eating in between. Open Monday–Saturday 10:00–21:00 and Sunday 11:00–19:00. If you want the difference between the food hall and the marketplace around it, our Quincy Market vs Faneuil Hall comparison explains it in one paragraph.

Newbury Street, Prudential Center and Copley Place

For proper retail, head to Back Bay: Newbury Street is eight blocks of high-end retailers and independent boutiques, and the Prudential Center (800 Boylston Street, Monday–Saturday 11:00–21:00, Sunday 11:00–20:00) connects by a glass skywalk to Copley Place. Both are a short Green Line ride west from Government Center or Haymarket.

Buying clothes tax-free up to $175

Massachusetts exempts clothing and footwear up to $175 per item from the 6.25% sales tax; only the amount above $175 is taxed, and athletic or protective-only wear is excluded. For visitors buying shoes or a coat, that is a real saving at the till.

Food shopping to take home

The Boston Public Market is a reliable place to buy edible souvenirs — maple syrup, honey, cheese, coffee and seafood from New England producers — and it validates parking and is open daily. Haymarket, by contrast, is for fresh produce to eat during your stay, not to pack in a suitcase.

Getting to the markets

Almost everything on this page sits on the MBTA subway, which Boston calls the T. A one-way subway fare is $2.40 and a local bus fare $1.70; a 1-Day Pass is $11.00 and a 7-Day Pass $22.50, payable with a CharlieCard, CharlieTicket, contactless card or phone.

For Quincy Market, Boston Public Market and Haymarket, three stops are within a block: Green Line Haymarket or Government Center, Blue Line Aquarium and Orange Line State Street or Haymarket. Time Out Market is on the Green Line D at Fenway; Hub Hall is at North Station; High Street Place is a short walk from South Station and Downtown Crossing; SoWa is on the Silver Line (Washington Street to Union Park Street) and the Boston Public Market sits on top of Haymarket on the Green and Orange lines. Full directions, walking times and the Logan Airport route are in How to Get Here, and garage rates and booking tips are on the Parking page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best farmers market near Quincy Market?

Haymarket, on Blackstone Street directly beside Quincy Market — it is the oldest outdoor market in the city, runs Fridays and Saturdays all year, and its pushcart vendors sell produce more cheaply than the downtown supermarkets. If you want an indoor, all-week alternative one block away, the Boston Public Market at 100 Hanover Street is open daily.

Is there a flea market near me in Boston?

The SoWa Vintage Market at 450 Harrison Avenue is Boston's weekly flea-style market, open every Sunday 11:00–16:00 all year, and free. In summer it is joined on Thayer Street by the Sunday SoWa Open Market (11:00–17:00, May to mid-November). For antiques, the Cambridge Antique Market across the river is open Tuesday–Sunday 11:00–18:00.

Does Boston have a night market?

Not a permanent one. Boston has recurring summer evening markets instead: the Night Bazaar at the Charles River Speedway (Thursdays and Fridays 17:00–22:00, free, monthly in June, July and August) and a one-night Chinatown Night Market on the Greenway in September. Both are event-based, so check the current season's dates.

Where should I eat in Boston if I want good food cheaply?

Start at Quincy Market. Entry is free, counter stalls run about $8–15 per person and sit-down restaurants roughly $10–18, and you can eat across a wide range of cuisines in one building. Boston Public Market, Hub Hall and Time Out Market all work the same way — order at separate counters, pay separately, seat yourself.

Are the markets open on Sundays?

Quincy Market is open on Sundays (11:00–19:00 for the marketplace, with the food hall typically closing earlier), as are Boston Public Market, Time Out Market and Hub Hall. High Street Place is closed on Sundays. Among the seasonal markets, Sunday is the big day for SoWa and the Greenway Artisan Market in season.

Summary: Eat at Quincy Market — free entry, over 25 eateries, $8–18 a head — and cross the street to Boston Public Market for New England produce. Shop for souvenirs from the 40-plus carts at Faneuil Hall Marketplace, or go to Back Bay's Newbury Street and Prudential Center for real retail, remembering that clothing up to $175 per item is tax-free. For fresh food, time your visit to a Friday or Saturday (Haymarket, all year), a Sunday (SoWa Open Market, May–November, and SoWa Vintage Market, year round), or a summer Thursday or Friday evening for the Night Bazaar. Everything is on the T, and three stations sit within a block of Quincy Market.
