Haymarket Boston — Which Days, What to Buy, and Whether It Is Worth the Walk

Hours, parking rates and market rules are the operator's and the City of Boston's own statements, read 21 November 2026. Prices change with the load on the day.

Haymarket is Boston's open-air produce market: every Friday and Saturday, all year, on Blackstone Street between Hanover Street and North Street. There is no building. Stalls of fruit, vegetables and fish go up on the street in the block north of Quincy Market, and every one of them is packed up at the end of the day. The Haymarket Pushcart Association, which runs it, calls it Boston's oldest outdoor market.

That is the difference worth knowing before you walk over. Quincy Market is a food hall you visit; Haymarket is a market you shop. It is worth the walk in one direction and a waste of it in the other, and the next few sections tell you which is which.

If you only want the answer: Friday and Saturday only, 4 AM–7 PM, on Blackstone Street between Hanover and North. Cash is the assumption. Walk over for produce, fish or flowers you intend to cook that week or carry home — not for a souvenir.

Days and Hours

Haymarket trades two days a week, every week of the year: Friday and Saturday. It is not a seasonal market and not a daily one, and the season is not in doubt — the City of Boston states the days as year-round on its own food resources page, last updated 12 November 2026.

The trading window is 4 AM–7 PM, which is the City's statement of the market's hours. The operator describes its own day as starting earlier on a Friday, from 3:00 AM, because that is when the stalls are set up — so a Friday at 3:30 AM looks open before the published window does. Saturday's start matches: 4 AM in both statements.

Haymarket trading days and hours — City of Boston window and the operator's own statement
Day Trading window (City of Boston) Operator's own hours (Haymarket Pushcart Association)
Friday 4 AM–7 PM, year-round From 3:00 AM to close
Saturday 4 AM–7 PM, year-round From 4:00 AM to close

The operator's hours run "to close" rather than to a fixed time, and its own shopping advice is what to plan around: the best produce is early and the best deals are later on Saturday. A Friday morning is for the full range, a Saturday afternoon for the markdowns.

Both the City and the association state every Friday and Saturday, year-round, and neither lists a holiday exception. Christmas Day 2026 falls on a Friday, so if that is your date, check the association's Instagram account (@haymarketboston) before you set out.

What Is Sold, and Who Sells It

Over 40 independent vendors trade here, according to the association. The City's own summary of the stock is "highly discounted seafood and produce", and the association describes the market as fresh produce, fish and flowers. The City's pushcart application lists the licensed product classes as fruits and vegetables, fish, and shellfish — which is the same short list in bureaucratic form.

The vendor mix has changed a lot in a generation. The Boston Globe reported on 25 May 2026 that vendors from 20 different countries now sell fruit, vegetables and seafood here on Fridays and Saturdays, where most of the 20th-century traders were Italian. The association itself dates from 1820.

It is a street of stalls, not a market hall: there is no seating, no cover and nothing to do but buy. What Quincy Market contains, counter by counter, is on what is inside Quincy Market; the wider picture of Boston's markets is on Boston food and markets.

What It Costs

Prices at Haymarket are quoted in pounds per dollar. The association's own shopper rules use "3 pounds per dollar" as the example — three pounds of that produce for USD 1. Read the numbers that way and the market makes sense immediately; the figure changes with the stall and the day, so a stall's price is a price for today's load.

Two things follow from that. The stock is genuinely cheap — the City's phrase is "highly discounted" — but the price is set per stall and per load, so the number you paid last time is not the number you will pay today. And haggling has a time: the association's rules say Saturday after 3 PM is the only time haggling is allowed, which is the same window in which the best deals are on.

Cash Is the Assumption

The City's own line on Haymarket is "Bring cash!". The association's shopper rules assume it too, down to the denomination: if you are buying about USD 1 of produce, pay with a USD 1 or a USD 5 bill. Come with small notes and you will not be the person holding up a queue of regulars.

Cash is the assumption at the stalls, and that is as far as it goes — plan on paying in notes and you will never be stuck.

Getting There

Haymarket station is the stop, on the Orange and Green lines. The market is in the block of Blackstone Street between Hanover and North Streets, which the association's own directions describe as just north of Quincy Market. The route on foot, the other stations and the walking times are on how to get to Quincy Market.

If you are arriving by bus, the busway at the station has been closed since June 2021 and the seven routes that used it now set down on Surface Road and Congress Street instead, as the MBTA's Haymarket project page states and Axios Boston reported again on 8 April 2026.

Parking

The association names Parcel 7 Garage, 136 Blackstone Street as the validated parking for the market, and publishes the validation rates as USD 1 for up to 2 hours and USD 3 for up to 3 hours. Those are validated rates, not the garage's drive-up tariff, which is higher — have the ticket validated. Two hours covers a shop at Haymarket and a walk through Quincy Market, and with validation it is a trivial cost for a market run.

Is It Worth the Walk?

Yes, for one kind of visit: produce, fish and flowers you intend to cook or carry home. Prices are quoted in pounds per dollar — 3 pounds for USD 1 in the association's rules — so a carrier bag of produce is small change, and the Globe's May 2026 feature found customers describing the stock as fresh and the market as somewhere they shop every weekend. The association's president emeritus also answers the old reputation directly — the days of mushy produce from the back of the pile are, in his words, gone.

No, for the other kind: a souvenir stop. Nothing here is sold as a souvenir, there is no seating, and cash is the assumption at every stall. If you are in a hotel room without a kitchen and flying home on Sunday, this is not your market.

And it is not a substitute for Quincy Market — it is the other half of the same morning. Quick Answers

What days is Haymarket open?

Friday and Saturday, every week of the year. Not seasonal, not daily. Both the City of Boston and the Haymarket Pushcart Association state the two days as year-round.

What time does Haymarket open and close?

The trading window is 4 AM–7 PM on both days, in the City of Boston's statement. The operator runs a Friday set-up from 3:00 AM, so the street looks open before the published window does on a Friday; Saturday starts at 4 AM either way. The operator gives its closing time only as "close".

Do the stalls take cards?

Cash is the assumption. The City's advice is "Bring cash!" and the association's rules are written around small notes — bring USD 1 and USD 5 bills.

Where do I park for Haymarket?

Parcel 7 Garage at 136 Blackstone Street, the validated garage the association names: USD 1 for up to 2 hours and USD 3 for up to 3 hours with validation — the garage's drive-up tariff is higher.

How do I get to Haymarket by public transport?

Haymarket station on the Orange and Green lines; the market is on Blackstone Street between Hanover and North Streets. The station's busway has been closed since June 2021, and the seven bus routes that used it stop on Surface Road and Congress Street instead.

Is Haymarket worth visiting?

For produce, fish and flowers you will cook or take home, yes — that is what the market is for. As a souvenir stop or a place to sit down, no: it is an open-air street market with no seating, trading on Friday and Saturday only.
