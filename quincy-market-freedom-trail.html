Is Quincy Market on the Freedom Trail? The Stop, the Route and What's Free

Last updated: October 2026. Checked against the Freedom Trail Foundation, the National Park Service and the sites' own pages on 27 October 2026. Admission prices are deliberately not listed — they change, so check each site before you go.

Quincy Market is not one of the 16 official Freedom Trail sites. The official site in this square is Faneuil Hall — the red-brick building immediately across the cobblestones from Quincy Market's central doors. The market sits directly on the route between two official stops, so you walk the trail through the marketplace either way; the building that carries the official listing is the one next door. This page settles that question, then gives you the route, what is free, and where the red line takes you next.

Quick facts: Freedom Trail: 2.5 miles, 16 official sites · Official site in this square: Faneuil Hall · Quincy Market: on the route, not on the list · Entry to Quincy Market and Faneuil Hall: free · Free ranger talks in the Great Hall Wed–Sun · Nearest T: State, Haymarket, Government Center · Faneuil Hall access: ramp and elevator, south side.

The short answer: Quincy Market is not on the 16-site list

The Freedom Trail Foundation publishes its official historic sites as a list of 16. That list names Boston Common, the Massachusetts State House, Park Street Church, Granary Burying Ground, King's Chapel and its burying ground, the Boston Latin School site with the Benjamin Franklin statue, the Old Corner Bookstore, the Old South Meeting House, the Old State House, the Boston Massacre Site, Faneuil Hall, the Paul Revere House, the Old North Church, Copp's Hill Burying Ground, USS Constitution and the Bunker Hill Monument. Quincy Market is not among them — the site listed for this square is Faneuil Hall.

That is worth saying plainly, because the internet is confidently split on it. Wikipedia's Quincy Market article states that "Quincy Market is a stop on the Freedom Trail", and visitor guides repeat the line. The Foundation's own Faneuil Hall page is more precise in the other direction, describing Quincy Market and Faneuil Hall Marketplace as adjacent to historic Faneuil Hall — a neighbouring complex, not the listed site.

Both things people feel to be true are true, which is why the confusion persists. The company that operates the marketplace says Faneuil Hall Marketplace "is located on Boston's historic Freedom Trail" — and it is: the red brick line runs across the square in front of the market. What the marketplace is not is one of the 16 official sites — and that decides which building you should go inside: Faneuil Hall, whose Great Hall is free to walk into.

Where the Freedom Trail runs through the square

The Foundation describes the trail as "a red-bricked path on City of Boston sidewalks" leading to its 16 official sites. Through this square it arrives from the south, past the Old State House and the Boston Massacre Site on Congress Street, crosses the cobblestones of Faneuil Hall Square between Quincy Market and Faneuil Hall, and leaves north toward the Paul Revere House. The National Park Service's own directions say to enter Faneuil Hall "through the main central doors across from Quincy Market" — so from under the market's central colonnade, the official stop of this square is directly opposite you. The trail neither detours to Quincy Market nor skips it; it runs between two official stops, which is why so many visitors assume the market is one of them.

On admission the National Park Service draws the clearest line: no entrance pass is required for Boston National Historical Park, and "there are no fees at the federally or municipally owned historic sites", which it lists as including Faneuil Hall, Bunker Hill Monument, USS Constitution and Dorchester Heights. The sites that do charge are run by independent, self-supporting organisations: the Old State House and Old South Meeting House on one joint ticket, and the Paul Revere House, Old North Church and King's Chapel separately. The Foundation's FAQ puts it simply: you do not have to pay to visit the trail, but select sites, and the Foundation's own tours, carry admission fees.

Walking the trail from Quincy Market

You are already on the line, so this is short. Distances below are the Freedom Trail Foundation's own figures from its site pages, plus the Paul Revere House's own walking note:

  1. In the square (0 minutes): stand between Quincy Market and Faneuil Hall. Faneuil Hall is the official stop and it is free — Great Hall on the second floor, the National Park Service visitor centre on the market (first) floor and lower level.
  2. North to the Paul Revere House (about 5–10 minutes): leave the square on the north side and follow the red brick line into the North End. The Foundation lists the Paul Revere House 0.4 miles / 9 minutes from Faneuil Hall; the Revere House's own directions call it a ten-minute walk from Faneuil Hall or Quincy Market. The two official figures disagree slightly — treat the leg as 5 to 10 minutes and trust the brick line.
  3. On to the Old North Church (about 5 minutes): the Foundation lists it 0.3 miles / 5 minutes beyond the Paul Revere House, then Copp's Hill Burying Ground, the last stop in the North End.
  4. Across to Charlestown: the trail crosses the river to USS Constitution and finishes at Bunker Hill Monument. Walking the other way instead brings you to the Boston Massacre Site (0.2 miles / 4 minutes) and the Old State House.

What's free at and around Quincy Market

  • Quincy Market and the marketplace: free to walk in — no ticket, no entry charge. You pay only for food and shopping (see our Complete Food Guide with Price List).
  • Faneuil Hall: free admission on all floors, per the National Park Service.
  • Free ranger talks in the Great Hall: about 15 minutes each, Wednesday–Sunday at 10:30am, 11:30am, 12:30pm, 1:30pm, 2:30pm and 3:30pm, year-round when the hall is not closed for City-sponsored events.
  • The NPS visitor centre: market (first) floor and lower level of Faneuil Hall — where to pick up trail maps and ask what is on that day. There are no fees for ranger-guided tours, talks or education programs in the park.
  • Access: Faneuil Hall has a ramp and elevator on the south side. Elsewhere expect cobblestones, brick sidewalks and hills — the Foundation's access page recommends a power wheelchair.

Ranger tours and getting to the square

The National Park Service says ranger-led tours of the Freedom Trail begin at Faneuil Hall seasonally, and its free Great Hall talks run year-round. The Freedom Trail Foundation separately runs its own ticketed walking tours with costumed guides — its main "Walk Into History" tour covers 11 of the 16 official sites. Those are not the same thing as the free ranger programs; see the Foundation's tours page for times and tickets.

Getting here is the easy part, and it is the same answer as how to get to Quincy Market: the operator notes the marketplace "is a short walk from the State, Haymarket, and Government Center MBTA stations", all within a block or two of the square. That means you can start the trail at Faneuil Hall without a car, finish at Bunker Hill and ride the T back. Full T, airport and garage detail is on our public transport, Getting Here and parking pages. Frequently asked questions

Is Quincy Market on the Freedom Trail?

Not as one of the official sites. The Foundation lists 16 and Quincy Market is not among them — the official site in this square is Faneuil Hall, immediately across the cobblestones. The market is on the trail's route, between the Boston Massacre Site and the Paul Revere House, so you walk through it either way. Wikipedia and other sources still call the market a trail stop; the Foundation's site list is the authority.

Is it free to visit Faneuil Hall?

Yes — the National Park Service states "free admission on all floors", and ranger programs there are free. The Old State House and Old South Meeting House (one joint ticket), the Paul Revere House, Old North Church and King's Chapel charge admission; check each site's own page, as prices change.

What time are the free ranger talks in the Great Hall?

Wednesday through Sunday at 10:30am, 11:30am, 12:30pm, 1:30pm, 2:30pm and 3:30pm. Each lasts about 15 minutes and they run year-round except when the hall is closed for City-sponsored events. The hall is on the second floor; enter through the main central doors across from Quincy Market, or take the elevator from the market floor.

Where does the Freedom Trail go after Quincy Market?

North: the Paul Revere House, about 5–10 minutes along the red brick line into the North End, then the Old North Church and Copp's Hill Burying Ground. South: the Boston Massacre Site (about 4 minutes) and the Old State House. The full trail runs 2.5 miles from Boston Common to Bunker Hill Monument.

Do I need a ticket to walk the Freedom Trail?

No. The Foundation's FAQ says you do not have to pay to visit the trail; select historic sites charge admission, and the Foundation's own guided tours are ticketed. Walking the brick line is free.
